Face detection and analysis with Azure AI Face, and the Limited Access policy for identification and verification

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Azure AI Face is the Azure AI service for detecting and analyzing human faces in images. It is a good example of how capability and responsibility go together in AI-900: you need to know what the service does and which parts are restricted.

Face detection finds faces in an image and returns a bounding box for each one. It can also return landmarks (points such as the corners of the eyes and the tip of the nose) and a set of attributes that describe the image of the face rather than the person: head pose (how the head is turned), whether the person is wearing glasses or a mask, whether the face is blurred, how well it is exposed, whether parts are occluded, and quality for recognition. Detection is useful for tasks such as counting faces, cropping profile photos, blurring faces for privacy or checking that a photo meets ID photo requirements.

Face recognition compares faces. Verification checks whether two faces belong to the same person (one-to-one), for example matching a selfie to an ID photo. Identification finds who a face belongs to by searching a group of known people (one-to-many), for example matching a person at a door to a list of employees. Recognition needs a face template stored for each known person.

Because recognition can be misused for surveillance or tracking, Microsoft makes face identification and verification Limited Access features. A customer must apply, describe the use case, be approved and agree to terms before using them. Some detection attributes are restricted in a similar way.

Microsoft also retired Face capabilities that inferred emotional states (such as happy or angry) and identity attributes such as gender and age, along with others like smile and facial hair. These were removed because the scientific basis for inferring emotion from facial expressions is weak and the attributes could enable discrimination. If a question proposes using Azure AI Face to detect customers' emotions, that is no longer available.

Facial analysis should always consider consent, privacy law, fairness across demographic groups and transparency to the people being analyzed. When a scenario only needs to count or locate people without faces, people detection in Azure AI Vision may be a less sensitive choice.

Key terms

Face detection
Finding faces in an image and returning their location and image attributes.
Face verification
Checking whether two face images belong to the same person (one-to-one).
Face identification
Finding which known person a face belongs to from a group (one-to-many).
Facial landmarks
Points on a face, such as eye corners and nose tip, returned by face detection.
Real-world example

A photo-ID kiosk uses face detection to check that each passport photo has one face, facing forward, not blurred and without glasses. The same company's plan to verify travellers against their passport photos requires an approved Limited Access application first.

Exam tip: Detection and image attributes such as blur, glasses and head pose: available. Identification and verification: Limited Access. Emotion, gender and age: retired.

Check yourself

What is the difference between verification and identification?

Verification compares two faces to confirm they are the same person (one-to-one); identification searches a group to find who someone is (one-to-many).

Why did Microsoft retire emotion inference from Azure AI Face?

Because inferring emotions from facial expressions lacks a reliable scientific basis and carries a high risk of misuse and unfair outcomes.
