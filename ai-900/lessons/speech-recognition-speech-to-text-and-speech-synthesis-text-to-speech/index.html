Speech recognition (speech to text) and speech synthesis (text to speech) with Azure AI Speech

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Azure AI Speech is the Azure AI service for working with spoken language. Its two core capabilities are speech recognition, which turns audio into text, and speech synthesis, which turns text into audio. Many voice solutions use both.

Speech to text (speech recognition) converts spoken audio into written text. The service uses an acoustic model that maps audio signals to sounds (phonemes) and a language model that maps sounds to the most likely words and sentences. It can transcribe in real time from a microphone or stream, which suits live captions and voice commands, or in batch from stored audio files, which suits transcribing recorded calls or meetings at scale. Output can include punctuation, timestamps and, with diarization, which speaker said what.

When your audio includes specialist vocabulary (product names, medical terms) or challenging acoustic conditions, you can improve accuracy with custom speech by providing sample audio and text to adapt the model. Typical scenarios: meeting transcripts, captioning videos, call centre analytics, dictation and voice-controlled apps.

Text to speech (speech synthesis) converts text into spoken audio. Azure AI Speech offers a large range of neural voices across many languages and locales, which sound natural and handle intonation well. You can control pronunciation, speaking rate, pitch, pauses and speaking style with Speech Synthesis Markup Language (SSML), an XML-based markup. Typical scenarios: voice assistants, reading content aloud for accessibility, audio announcements and generating narration. Custom neural voice, which creates a voice resembling a specific person, is a Limited Access feature.

Speech also offers speech translation (covered with Translator), speaker recognition and other capabilities. You create an Azure AI Speech resource (single-service, with a free F0 tier where available, or through a multi-service resource) and try features in the speech playground in the Foundry portal or Speech Studio before using the SDK or REST API.

The exam trap is direction. Audio in, text out is speech to text. Text in, audio out is text to speech. A voice bot usually needs both: speech to text to hear the user and text to speech to reply.

Key terms

Speech to text
Converting spoken audio into written text; also called speech recognition.
Text to speech
Converting text into spoken audio; also called speech synthesis.
Neural voice
A natural-sounding synthetic voice produced by a deep learning model.
SSML
Speech Synthesis Markup Language, used to control pronunciation, rate, pitch and style of synthesized speech.
Real-world example

A bank's phone line uses speech to text to understand callers who say 'check my balance', looks up the balance, and replies with text to speech in a neural voice. Recorded calls are transcribed overnight in batch for quality review, with custom speech trained on the bank's product names.

Exam tip: Always check the direction: captions and transcripts are speech to text; reading text aloud is text to speech. Custom neural voice needs Limited Access approval.

Check yourself

A podcast host wants written transcripts of every episode. Which capability?

Speech to text (speech recognition), transcribing the audio files in batch.

What is SSML used for?

To control how text to speech sounds, such as pronunciation, speed, pitch, pauses and speaking style.
