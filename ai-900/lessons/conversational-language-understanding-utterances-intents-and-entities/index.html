Conversational language understanding: utterances, intents and entities

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

When people talk to an app in their own words, the app must work out what they want and pull out the details it needs. Conversational language understanding (CLU), a feature of Azure AI Language, does this. It is the successor to the older Language Understanding (LUIS) service, which was retired.

CLU is built on three concepts. An utterance is something a user might say or type, such as 'Book a table for four at 7pm tomorrow' or 'What's the weather like in Madrid?'. An intent is the goal or action the user wants, such as BookTable or GetWeather. An entity is a piece of information in the utterance that the app needs to act, such as the party size (four), the time (7pm tomorrow) or the location (Madrid).

To build a CLU model, you create a project in Language Studio, define the intents and entities your app cares about, and add example utterances for each intent, labeling the entities in them. Good practice includes many varied utterances per intent and a None intent for things the app should not handle. You then train the model, review its evaluation results, and deploy it. Entities can be learned from labeled examples, or defined as lists of known values or with prebuilt components for common types like numbers and dates.

At runtime, your app sends the user's utterance to the deployed model. The response contains the top intent with a confidence score and the entities it found. The app then acts: calling a booking system with the extracted values, asking a follow-up question if an entity is missing, or passing the conversation elsewhere if confidence is low.

CLU does not produce the reply text itself and does not look up answers from a knowledge base. It turns natural language into structured data (intent plus entities) that your code acts on. It is commonly used inside bots, voice assistants and apps with a natural language command box, often combined with speech to text for voice input.

Know the vocabulary precisely: utterance is the input, intent is the goal, entity is a detail. Exam questions often give a sentence and ask which part is which.

Key terms

Utterance
An example of something a user might say or type to an app.
Intent
The goal or action a user wants, predicted from an utterance.
Entity
A specific detail in an utterance that the app needs, such as a date, place or quantity.
Conversational language understanding (CLU)
An Azure AI Language feature that predicts intents and extracts entities from user input.
Real-world example

A smart home app receives 'Turn off the kitchen lights in ten minutes'. CLU returns the intent TurnOff with 0.96 confidence and the entities device = lights, room = kitchen and time = in ten minutes. The app schedules the action through the home automation API.

Exam tip: In 'Order two large pizzas', the whole sentence is the utterance, OrderPizza is the intent, and 'two' and 'large' are entities. CLU returns structured intent and entities, not an answer.

Check yourself

In 'Cancel my 3pm meeting with Sam', identify the intent and entities.

The intent is CancelMeeting; the entities are 3pm (time) and Sam (person).

Why add a None intent to a CLU project?

To catch utterances the app should not handle, so they are not forced into a wrong intent.
