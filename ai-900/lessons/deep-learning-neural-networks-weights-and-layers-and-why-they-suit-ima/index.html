Deep learning: neural networks, weights and layers, and why they suit images, speech and language

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Deep learning is a branch of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with many layers. It is behind nearly all modern computer vision, speech recognition and language models, including the large language models used in generative AI.

A neural network is loosely inspired by the brain. It is made of layers of simple units (artificial neurons). Each neuron takes numbers from the previous layer, multiplies each by a weight, adds them up with a bias value, and passes the result through an activation function that decides how strongly it fires. The first layer receives the input features, such as pixel values; the last layer produces the output, such as a probability for each class. The layers in between are hidden layers, and a network with many of them is called deep.

Training adjusts the weights. The network makes predictions on training data, a loss function measures how wrong they are, and an optimization method (gradient descent, using a technique called backpropagation) nudges every weight in the direction that reduces the loss. This is repeated over many passes through the data (epochs) until the predictions are good. Large networks have millions or billions of weights, which is why training needs lots of data and powerful hardware such as GPUs.

The great strength of deep learning is that it learns useful features automatically. With classic machine learning, a person often had to design features by hand, for example 'number of words in capital letters'. A deep network fed raw pixels learns for itself that early layers should detect edges, later layers shapes and later still whole objects. That makes it ideal for unstructured data such as images, audio and text, where good features are hard to design by hand.

Different architectures suit different data. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) are designed for images. Transformers, covered in the next lesson, dominate language and are increasingly used for images and audio too. Deep learning models can still do regression and classification; they are simply a more powerful kind of model.

For AI-900 you do not need the mathematics. Remember that neural networks are layers of weighted connections, training adjusts the weights to reduce error, and deep learning excels when data is complex and plentiful.

Key terms

Neural network
A model made of layers of connected artificial neurons whose weights are learned during training.
Weight
A number on a connection between neurons that is adjusted during training to reduce error.
Deep learning
Machine learning with neural networks that have many hidden layers.
Loss function
A calculation that measures how far a model's predictions are from the correct answers.
Real-world example

A postal service trains a deep neural network on millions of images of handwritten postcodes. Nobody writes rules for loops and strokes; the early layers learn edges, later layers learn digit shapes, and the output layer gives a probability for each digit.

Exam tip: Deep learning is a subset of machine learning, which is a subset of AI. If a scenario involves images, audio or natural language at scale, deep learning is the technique underneath.

Check yourself

What is adjusted when a neural network is trained?

The weights (and biases) on the connections between neurons, to reduce the loss.

Why is deep learning well suited to images?

It learns useful features such as edges and shapes directly from raw pixels instead of needing hand-designed features.
