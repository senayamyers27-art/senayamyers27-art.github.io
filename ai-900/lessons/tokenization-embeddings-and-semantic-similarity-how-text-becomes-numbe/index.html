Tokenization, embeddings and semantic similarity: how text becomes numbers

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Machine learning models work with numbers, not words. Every NLP system, from a sentiment classifier to a large language model, must first turn text into numeric form. AI-900 expects you to understand the main steps conceptually: tokenization, frequency-based representations and embeddings.

Tokenization splits text into tokens, the units a model processes. Simple approaches split on spaces and punctuation, so 'The cat sat.' becomes 'The', 'cat', 'sat', '.'. Modern language models use subword tokenization, which breaks rare words into common pieces so the vocabulary stays manageable while any word can still be represented. Before or during tokenization, text may be normalized, for example converted to lowercase, and very common stop words such as 'the' or 'a' may be removed for simpler statistical models.

Early NLP represented text by counting. Term frequency counts how often each word appears in a document. Term frequency-inverse document frequency (TF-IDF) weighs words that appear often in one document but rarely across the collection, so distinctive words stand out. N-grams capture short sequences such as 'not good', which carry meaning single words miss. These representations power simple classifiers and key phrase techniques but do not capture meaning well: 'car' and 'automobile' look completely unrelated.

Embeddings solve that. An embedding is a vector (a list of many numbers) learned by a model so that texts with similar meanings are close together in vector space. Word, sentence and document embeddings all exist. Because meaning is encoded as position, you can measure semantic similarity mathematically, typically with cosine similarity, the angle between two vectors. 'How do I reset my password?' and 'I forgot my login details' share few words but have similar embeddings.

Embeddings enable semantic search (finding documents by meaning rather than exact keywords), recommendations, clustering of similar texts and the retrieval step in retrieval augmented generation. Azure OpenAI offers embeddings models for this purpose, and Azure AI Search can store vectors and run vector searches.

A language model, in general terms, is a model that has learned the statistical patterns of a language: which tokens tend to follow which, and what they mean in context. Tokens and embeddings are the foundation on which both traditional NLP features and generative AI are built.

Key terms

Tokenization
Splitting text into tokens such as words or subword pieces for a model to process.
TF-IDF
A weighting that scores words by how frequent they are in a document and how rare across the collection.
Embedding
A vector that represents the meaning of text so similar meanings are close together.
Semantic similarity
How close two texts are in meaning, often measured with cosine similarity between embeddings.
Real-world example

An IT help desk converts every past ticket and knowledge article into embeddings. When a user types 'my laptop will not connect to the office network', semantic search finds the article titled 'Troubleshooting Wi-Fi authentication failures' even though the words barely overlap.

Exam tip: Counting words cannot tell that 'car' and 'automobile' are related; embeddings can. Semantic search and similarity questions point to embeddings.

Check yourself

What is the purpose of tokenization?

To split text into units (tokens) that can be mapped to numbers and processed by a model.

Why can embeddings find related documents that share no words?

Because they represent meaning as vectors, and texts with similar meaning have vectors that are close together.
