Summarization and custom text classification in Azure AI Language

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Beyond the prebuilt analysis features, Azure AI Language can condense long text into summaries and can learn your own categories for classifying text. These two capabilities appear in scenarios about handling large volumes of documents and messages.

Summarization produces a shorter version of a document or conversation that keeps the key points. Extractive summarization selects the most important sentences from the original text and returns them, each with a rank score, so the summary uses the author's exact words. Abstractive summarization generates new sentences that capture the main ideas, which usually reads more naturally, like a person writing a summary. Conversation summarization is designed for transcripts such as support calls or meetings and can produce summaries of the issue and resolution, or chapter titles for a long recording.

Summarization helps people deal with information overload: getting the gist of a long report, briefing an agent on a customer's previous calls, or creating a short preview for each article. It complements key phrase extraction, which returns separate phrases rather than readable sentences.

Custom text classification lets you train a model to assign your own labels to text. You define the classes, such as 'complaint', 'refund', 'order change' and 'feedback', upload example documents to a storage account, label them in Language Studio, train the model, review its evaluation metrics (precision, recall and F1 per class), and deploy it. Single-label classification assigns one class per document; multi-label classification can assign several, such as a movie synopsis labeled both 'comedy' and 'romance'.

Similarly, custom named entity recognition lets you train the service to extract your own entity types, such as contract clause types or internal part numbers, when the prebuilt entity categories do not cover them.

The pattern matches the rest of Azure AI: use prebuilt features first when they fit, and train a custom model only when your categories or entities are specific to your business. Customizing a model this way needs only labeled examples, not data science expertise. For open-ended summaries or rewriting in a specific style, a generative AI model is another option you will study in domain 5.

Key terms

Extractive summarization
Summarizing by selecting and returning the most important sentences from the original text.
Abstractive summarization
Summarizing by generating new sentences that capture the main ideas.
Custom text classification
Training Azure AI Language to assign your own categories to documents using labeled examples.
Multi-label classification
Classification in which one document can receive more than one label.
Real-world example

A council receives thousands of emails a week. A custom text classification model sorts them into 'waste collection', 'parking', 'council tax' and 'planning' for the right teams, and abstractive summarization gives each officer a two-sentence summary of long email threads.

Exam tip: Short version of a long text: summarization (extractive picks sentences, abstractive writes new ones). Your own categories: custom text classification trained on labeled examples.

Check yourself

What is the difference between extractive and abstractive summarization?

Extractive returns key sentences from the original text; abstractive generates new sentences that capture the meaning.

What do you need to train a custom text classification model?

Example documents labeled with your own classes, uploaded to storage and labeled in Language Studio.
