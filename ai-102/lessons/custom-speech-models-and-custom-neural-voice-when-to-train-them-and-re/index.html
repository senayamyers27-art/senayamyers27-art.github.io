Custom speech models and custom neural voice: when to train them and responsible use limits

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Prebuilt speech models work well for general conversation, but some situations need customization: recognition that struggles with specialist vocabulary or noisy environments, or a brand that wants its own distinctive synthetic voice. Azure AI Speech offers custom speech for recognition and custom neural voice for synthesis.

Custom speech adapts speech to text to your domain. First test the base model with your own audio to see whether customization is needed. Then add training data in Speech Studio. Plain text data (sentences containing your terms, such as product names and jargon) improves recognition of vocabulary and is the cheapest to produce. Structured text can describe patterns and pronunciations. Audio with human-labeled transcripts teaches the model your acoustic conditions, such as factory noise, accents or a phone codec, and gives the biggest gains for hard audio. Pronunciation files can map spoken forms to display forms.

Measure accuracy with word error rate (WER): the number of substitutions, deletions and insertions divided by the number of words in the reference transcript. Lower is better. Compare the base and custom models on the same test set before switching. Deploy the custom model to a custom endpoint and point your app's SpeechConfig at its endpoint ID. For lighter needs, a phrase list added at runtime boosts specific words without training.

Custom neural voice creates a synthetic voice that sounds like a specific voice actor, for brand assistants or characters. Because a realistic voice clone could be misused for impersonation or fraud, it is a Limited Access feature: you must apply and be approved, and you must obtain a recorded consent statement from the voice talent, in which they acknowledge that their voice will be used to create a synthetic voice. The service checks this recording against the training audio. Training requires high-quality studio recordings with matching scripts. There is also personal voice, which creates a voice from a short sample for approved scenarios, with similar consent requirements.

Responsible use goes beyond approval. Disclose to listeners that a voice is synthetic where appropriate, do not use a voice beyond the scope the talent agreed to, and protect the voice model like any sensitive asset. Microsoft's transparency notes and code of conduct describe prohibited uses.

The decision: use prebuilt models first; add a phrase list for a few terms; train custom speech when vocabulary or acoustics still cause errors; and consider custom neural voice only when a unique brand voice justifies the approval process and cost.

Key terms

Custom speech
A speech to text model adapted with your text and audio data for better accuracy in your domain.
Word error rate (WER)
The share of words substituted, deleted or inserted compared with a reference transcript.
Custom neural voice
A Limited Access feature that trains a synthetic voice resembling a specific consenting voice talent.
Phrase list
A runtime list of words that boosts their recognition without training a custom model.
Real-world example

A steel plant's voice logging app had a WER of 25% because of machine noise and alloy names. Training custom speech with 10 hours of floor recordings and their transcripts plus a list of alloy names brought WER down to 9%, measured on a held-out test set.

Exam tip: Vocabulary problems: plain text data or a phrase list. Noise or accent problems: audio plus human-labeled transcripts. Custom neural voice needs Limited Access approval and the talent's recorded consent.

Check yourself

How is word error rate calculated?

Substitutions plus deletions plus insertions, divided by the number of words in the reference transcript.

What must you have from the voice talent before training custom neural voice?

A recorded consent statement from the talent, in addition to Microsoft's Limited Access approval.
