Analyzing video with Azure AI Video Indexer

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Video is hard to search: an hour of footage can hide the one sentence or scene you need. Azure AI Video Indexer analyzes video and audio files with many AI models at once and produces time-coded insights that make video searchable, accessible and easier to edit or moderate.

You create a Video Indexer account (an Azure resource connected to a storage account) and upload videos through the Video Indexer website, the REST API or by pointing it at a URL. Indexing runs asynchronously; when it finishes you can view insights in the portal, retrieve them as JSON through the API, or embed Video Indexer widgets (a player and an insights panel) in your own web pages.

Audio insights include a transcript with speaker identification, translation of the transcript into other languages, captions files, keywords, named entities (people, places, brands mentioned), topics, sentiment and audio effects such as applause or silence. Visual insights include OCR of on-screen text, labels for objects and actions, scenes and shots with keyframes, detected faces and, where approved, recognized faces or celebrities, plus detected brands and logos. Because everything is time-coded, you can jump to the moment a keyword was spoken or a product appeared, and build highlight reels.

Customization improves results for your content: custom language models teach the transcription your vocabulary, custom brand lists add your products, and custom person models (Limited Access) recognize specific people. Face identification and celebrity recognition in Video Indexer follow the same Limited Access rules as Azure AI Face.

Video Indexer suits recorded content libraries: training videos, broadcasts, meetings, marketing assets. For live video from cameras, other approaches apply: Microsoft announced the retirement of the Spatial Analysis container for people counting and movement, and newer solutions typically sample frames and send them to Image Analysis or a multimodal model, or use Azure AI Content Understanding for video extraction. There is also an Arc-enabled version of Video Indexer for running at the edge when video must stay on premises.

When choosing, remember that Custom Vision and Image Analysis work on still images, while Video Indexer handles whole videos with audio and visual insights together.

Key terms

Video Indexer
An Azure AI service that extracts time-coded audio and visual insights from video.
Insights
The JSON output of indexing, such as transcripts, OCR, labels, keywords, faces and scenes, each with timestamps.
Widgets
Embeddable player and insights components for showing indexed video in your own web app.
Keyframe
A representative frame chosen from a shot, used for thumbnails and visual analysis.
Real-world example

A university uploads 2,000 recorded lectures to Video Indexer. Students search for 'eigenvalue' and jump straight to the minute it was said or written on a slide, using the transcript and OCR insights, and captions in three languages come from the translated transcript.

Exam tip: Searchable transcripts, on-screen text, topics and scenes from recorded videos point to Video Indexer. Recognizing specific faces in video still requires Limited Access approval.

Check yourself

Name three audio insights and three visual insights Video Indexer can extract.

Audio: transcript, keywords, topics, sentiment, translation. Visual: OCR, labels, scenes and shots, faces, brands.

How can a web team show indexed videos with their insights in its own site?

By embedding Video Indexer's player and insights widgets, or by building a UI on the insights JSON from the API.
