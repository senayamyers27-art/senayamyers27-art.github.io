Choosing between prebuilt Image Analysis, Custom Vision and multimodal models for a vision task

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Azure gives you several ways to get answers from images, and many exam scenarios are really asking you to choose among them. The main options are prebuilt Image Analysis, a custom model trained with Custom Vision, a multimodal chat model, and specialist services such as Face, Document Intelligence and Video Indexer.

Prebuilt Image Analysis is the default for general visual understanding: captions, tags, common objects, people, smart crops and text. It needs no training data, costs little per image, returns consistent structured JSON and is fast. Its limit is vocabulary: it knows general concepts such as 'shoe' or 'dog', not your catalog numbers or your specific defect types.

Custom Vision fits when you need to recognize your own categories consistently at scale: specific products, parts, defects, plant diseases. It needs labeled images and a training cycle, but once trained it gives consistent, measurable results with precision and recall you can report, and compact models can be exported for offline use at the edge with no per-call cost.

Multimodal chat models such as GPT-4o accept an image with a question and answer in natural language or JSON. They are extremely flexible: they can describe complex scenes, read charts, compare images and follow instructions like 'list any safety hazards in this photo'. They need no training for a new question. The trade-offs are cost per image, latency, variability between runs, and harder evaluation: outputs are free text unless you enforce a schema, and the model may confidently misread details. They are ideal for low-volume, open-ended or rapidly changing questions and for prototyping.

Specialists win in their areas. Forms and invoices go to Document Intelligence for named fields. Faces go to Face, with its access controls. Recorded video goes to Video Indexer. Harmful image content goes to Content Safety.

A practical decision path: if a prebuilt feature answers the question, use it. If you need your own categories at high volume or offline, train Custom Vision. If the question is open-ended, changes often or needs reasoning, use a multimodal model, and consider combining: for example, Image Analysis to filter images cheaply, then a multimodal model only on the few that need deeper analysis.

Key terms

Prebuilt model
A model trained by Microsoft that works without your training data.
Custom model
A model trained on your own labeled data to recognize your specific categories.
Multimodal model
A model that accepts images with text and can reason and answer in natural language.
Edge deployment
Running a model on a local device rather than calling a cloud endpoint.
Real-world example

A property inspection startup uses Image Analysis to tag room types in thousands of listing photos, a Custom Vision object detector exported to tablets to find water damage offline during inspections, and a multimodal model to write a narrative report from the flagged photos for the inspector to review.

Exam tip: General concepts, no training: Image Analysis. Your own categories at scale or offline: Custom Vision. Open-ended questions or reasoning about an image: a multimodal model. Forms: Document Intelligence.

Check yourself

When is a multimodal model a worse choice than Custom Vision?

For high-volume, well-defined recognition of your own categories where you need consistent, measurable results, low cost per image or offline use.

A team needs to know whether a photo contains any animals. Which is the simplest option?

Prebuilt Image Analysis tags or objects, which recognize general concepts like animals without training.
