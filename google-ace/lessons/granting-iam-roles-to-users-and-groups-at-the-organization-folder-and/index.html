Granting IAM roles to users and groups at the organization, folder and project levels

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

In IAM, you grant a role to a principal on a resource. A principal can be a Google account, a Google group, a service account, a Cloud Identity or Workspace domain, or special identifiers such as allUsers. A role is a collection of permissions, such as compute.instances.start. The combination of principals and a role is a binding, and all bindings on a resource form its allow policy.

You can grant roles in the console on the IAM page of the organization, folder or project (Grant access), or on many individual resources such as a bucket or a service account. From the CLI you use commands like gcloud projects add-iam-policy-binding my-proj --member=group:devs@example.com --role=roles/compute.instanceAdmin.v1. There are matching commands for folders (gcloud resource-manager folders add-iam-policy-binding) and the organization (gcloud organizations add-iam-policy-binding). To remove a grant, use remove-iam-policy-binding with the same member and role.

Where you grant a role decides how far it reaches. A role granted at the organization applies to every project, which is right for a handful of central teams such as security reviewers or billing admins. A role granted on a folder covers every project in it, which suits a department or environment. Granting on a project or a single resource is the narrowest and best for least privilege. Always check the scope in a question: 'only this bucket' means grant on the bucket, not the project.

To review access, gcloud projects get-iam-policy my-proj prints the bindings, and the console's IAM page shows inherited grants too. Remember that what you see on a project may be only part of the story: a principal might also have roles inherited from a folder or the organization. Tools such as Policy Analyzer and Policy Troubleshooter help answer 'who has access to what' across the hierarchy.

Prefix members by type: user:, group:, serviceAccount: and domain:. A missing or wrong prefix is a common reason a gcloud grant fails.

Key terms

Principal
An identity that can be granted access: a user, group, service account, domain or special identifier.
Role binding
The pairing of a role with one or more principals in an allow policy.
Allow policy
The set of role bindings attached to a resource, also called an IAM policy.
add-iam-policy-binding
The gcloud subcommand that adds one member-role binding to a resource's policy.
Real-world example

The data team needs to run BigQuery jobs in the analytics project only. The admin runs gcloud projects add-iam-policy-binding analytics-prod --member=group:data-team@example.com --role=roles/bigquery.jobUser and grants BigQuery Data Viewer on one dataset, rather than giving the group a broad role on the whole organization.

Exam tip: Choose the lowest level that meets the requirement, grant to a group, and use a predefined role before a basic role. If the question mentions 'all projects in the department', a folder-level grant is usually the intended answer.

Check yourself

Which command removes a role from a group on a project?

gcloud projects remove-iam-policy-binding PROJECT --member=group:NAME@example.com --role=ROLE.

A user needs access to one bucket. Where should you grant the role?

On the bucket itself, so the access doesn't extend to other buckets in the project.
