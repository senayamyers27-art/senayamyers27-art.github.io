Domain 1: Project management concepts

  • Project basics: projects vs operations and programs, the triple constraint, and project roles (sponsor, project manager, PMO, team, stakeholders)
  • Change control process: change requests, impact assessment, change control board, approval, implementation and communicating changes
  • Types of change: scope creep, gold plating, timeline, budget, resource, requirements and emergency changes
  • Risk management activities: identification, qualitative and quantitative analysis, risk register, probability and impact matrix
  • Risk responses: avoid, mitigate, transfer, accept, escalate, exploit, enhance and share; contingency and management reserves
  • Communication management: communication plan, synchronous vs asynchronous methods, audience, frequency and tailoring
  • Stakeholder management: stakeholder register, power/interest grid, engagement and managing expectations
  • Resource management: resource types, allocation, leveling vs smoothing, shared resources and RACI charts
  • Schedule development: activity sequencing, dependencies (FS, SS, FF, SF), critical path, float, milestones, crashing and fast-tracking
  • Agile methodology: Scrum roles, events and artifacts, user stories, backlog refinement, velocity and Kanban
  • Other methodologies: waterfall (predictive), hybrid, PRINCE2, Lean and Six Sigma (DMAIC), and choosing an approach
  • Team dynamics and conflict resolution: team development stages, conflict techniques, motivation and running effective meetings

Domain 2: Project life cycle phases

  • Discovery and concept preparation: business case, feasibility, ROI and payback period, and preliminary scope
  • Initiating: project charter, sponsor authorization, high-level scope, assumptions, constraints and success criteria
  • Initiating: identifying stakeholders, assigning the project manager, and holding the kickoff meeting
  • Planning: requirements gathering, scope statement, work breakdown structure (WBS) and WBS dictionary
  • Planning: estimating time and cost (analogous, parametric, three-point/PERT, bottom-up) and setting baselines
  • Planning: budget development and procurement (make vs buy, RFI, RFP, RFQ, statement of work, contract types)
  • Planning: subsidiary plans for quality, risk, communication, transition and release
  • Executing: directing the work, managing vendors, quality assurance vs quality control, and deliverable acceptance
  • Monitoring and controlling: tracking progress against baselines, variance analysis, issue management and escalation
  • Closing: formal acceptance, transition to operations, contract closure, releasing resources, lessons learned and archiving

Domain 3: Tools and documentation

  • Project management software: scheduling tools, Gantt charts, Kanban boards, ticketing systems and dashboards
  • Collaboration tools: real-time vs asynchronous communication, shared workspaces, file sharing and document version control
  • Quality charts: Pareto, histogram, run and control charts, scatter diagrams and fishbone (Ishikawa) diagrams
  • Agile reporting: burndown and burnup charts, velocity charts and cumulative flow diagrams
  • Earned value management: PV, EV, AC, CV, SV, CPI, SPI, EAC and ETC, and interpreting the results
  • Core project documents: project management plan, schedule, RACI, risk register, issue log, change log and assumption log
  • Meeting and status documentation: agendas, minutes, action items, status reports and executive summaries
  • Vendor and procurement documents: NDA, MOU, SLA, SOW, purchase orders, invoices and change orders
  • Decision-making tools: SWOT, cost-benefit analysis, decision trees and expected monetary value, brainstorming and root cause analysis

Domain 4: Basics of IT and governance

  • Security concepts for IT projects: CIA triad, least privilege, access reviews and building security requirements in from the start
  • Data privacy and compliance: PII, PHI, data classification, retention, GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS and audits
  • IT infrastructure basics: servers, networks, storage and endpoints, and how infrastructure changes affect projects
  • Cloud and deployment models: on-premises, IaaS, PaaS, SaaS, public, private and hybrid cloud, and their trade-offs
  • Software development life cycle and DevOps: CI/CD, development, test, staging and production environments, and release management
  • Operational change management: change advisory board, maintenance windows, change freezes, rollback plans and downtime
  • Physical and environmental considerations: facility access, power and cooling, asset tracking and secure disposal
  • Governance: PMO standards, phase gate reviews, organizational change management, training and user adoption
  • Business continuity for IT projects: backups, disaster recovery, RTO and RPO, and testing before go-live
