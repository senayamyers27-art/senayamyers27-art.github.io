ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 2: Governance and management of IT

IT performance monitoring, reporting and quality management: KPIs, balanced scorecard, maturity

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Governance needs feedback. Leaders cannot direct IT well unless they know whether it is delivering what was promised, at what cost and with what risk. Performance monitoring provides that feedback through meaningful measures and regular reporting.

Key performance indicators (KPIs) measure how well a process is achieving its goals, such as percentage of changes causing incidents or system availability against target. Key risk indicators (KRIs) signal rising risk, such as the number of unpatched critical systems. Good metrics are linked to objectives, measurable from reliable data, and reported to people who can act on them. The IT balanced scorecard organizes measures into four perspectives: financial or business contribution, customer or user orientation, internal processes (operational excellence) and learning and growth (future orientation). It prevents IT from judging itself only on technical metrics.

Quality management ensures IT processes and products meet requirements consistently. Quality assurance defines and checks processes, while quality control inspects outputs. Standards such as ISO 9001 for quality management and ISO/IEC 20000 for IT service management provide structure. Capability or maturity models, such as the capability levels used in COBIT, rate processes from incomplete or ad hoc to optimized, helping organizations identify gaps between current and target capability and plan improvement.

Auditors check whether metrics are defined, accurate and reported to the right level, whether targets are realistic, and whether poor results trigger action. A common finding is a dashboard full of activity counts, such as tickets closed, that says little about business value or risk.

Key terms

Key performance indicator (KPI)
A measure of how well a process or activity is achieving its objective.
Key risk indicator (KRI)
A metric that signals increasing exposure to a particular risk.
Balanced scorecard
A performance tool that measures across financial, customer, internal process and learning and growth perspectives.
Capability level
A rating of how well a process is performed and managed, from incomplete to optimized.
Real-world example

A CIO's monthly report lists servers managed and help desk tickets closed. The auditor recommends adding measures tied to business goals, such as availability of revenue-critical systems, project benefits achieved and user satisfaction, arranged as a balanced scorecard.

Exam tip: Look for measures that show value and outcomes, not just activity. A balanced scorecard that links IT to business goals is usually the best answer for showing IT's contribution.

Check yourself

What is the difference between a KPI and a KRI?

A KPI measures performance toward a goal; a KRI signals increasing risk exposure.

What are the four balanced scorecard perspectives?

Financial (business contribution), customer (user orientation), internal process and learning and growth.
