ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 5: Protection of information assets

Security incident response management, evidence collection and forensics

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Even strong defenses will sometimes fail. Incident response management limits damage, restores operations and learns from each event. An organization that prepares in advance responds faster and more consistently than one that improvises.

Incident response follows a lifecycle. Preparation includes a policy and plan, a response team with defined roles, contact lists (legal, communications, management, law enforcement, regulators), tools and playbooks for common scenarios, and exercises. Detection and analysis confirm that an incident has occurred and assess its scope and severity. Containment stops the spread, for example by isolating hosts or disabling accounts. Eradication removes the cause, such as malware and attacker access. Recovery restores systems, for example from clean backups, and monitors for reinfection. The post-incident review captures lessons learned and improvements. Legal and regulatory requirements, including breach notification deadlines, must be built into the plan.

Evidence collection supports investigation and possible legal action. Evidence should be collected in order of volatility, starting with the most volatile, such as memory and network connections, before disk contents and archived data. Forensic copies should be bit-for-bit images, verified with hash values, and analysis should be done on copies, never the original. Chain of custody documentation records who collected, handled, transferred and stored each item, and when, so it can be shown that evidence was not altered. Forensic work requires trained staff or specialists; an untrained person can easily destroy evidence.

The auditor reviews whether the plan exists, is tested and current, whether incidents are logged and classified, whether lessons learned lead to changes, and whether evidence handling procedures are defined.

Key terms

Containment
Actions that limit the spread and impact of an incident.
Order of volatility
The sequence for collecting evidence, from most short-lived (memory) to most persistent (archives).
Chain of custody
Documentation showing who handled evidence, when and how, to prove it was not altered.
Forensic image
An exact bit-for-bit copy of storage media, verified with a hash, used for analysis.
Real-world example

After ransomware hits a file server, the team isolates it from the network, captures memory, images the disk and records hashes and chain of custody. They confirm backups are clean and the attacker's accounts are removed before restoring, then hold a lessons-learned review that leads to MFA on remote access.

Exam tip: Collect evidence in order of volatility, work on verified copies and keep chain of custody. Before restoring after ransomware, make sure the attacker's access is removed and backups are clean.

Check yourself

Why analyze a copy rather than the original evidence?

To preserve the original unaltered, so its integrity can be proven with hash values and chain of custody.

What is the purpose of the post-incident review?

To identify what worked, what did not and what to change to prevent or better handle future incidents.
