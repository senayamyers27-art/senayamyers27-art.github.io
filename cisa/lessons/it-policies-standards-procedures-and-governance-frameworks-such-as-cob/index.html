ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 2: Governance and management of IT

IT policies, standards, procedures and governance frameworks such as COBIT

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Policies turn governance intentions into rules. A clear document hierarchy lets everyone know what is required and lets auditors test against something concrete.

At the top, a policy is a high-level statement of management intent, approved by senior management, such as requiring all information to be protected according to its classification. Standards make policies specific and mandatory, for example requiring a particular encryption strength. Procedures give step-by-step instructions to carry out a task. Guidelines are recommended but optional practices. Baselines define minimum configurations for a particular platform. Policies should be reviewed regularly, at least annually or after significant change, communicated to staff, and enforced. An auditor looks for approval, currency, communication and evidence that practice matches the written word.

Governance frameworks give structure. COBIT, developed by ISACA, is a framework for the governance and management of enterprise information and technology. It separates governance objectives (evaluate, direct and monitor) from management objectives (align, plan and organize; build, acquire and implement; deliver, service and support; monitor, evaluate and assess). It uses design factors to tailor a governance system to each organization and capability levels to measure processes. Other frameworks complement it: ITIL for IT service management, ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management systems, and the NIST Cybersecurity Framework for organizing cybersecurity outcomes.

A policy that does not reflect reality is a risk, and so is a reality that has no policy. The auditor's job is to compare them, report gaps and check that exceptions are formally approved and tracked.

Key terms

Policy
A high-level, management-approved statement of intent and direction.
Standard
A mandatory, specific requirement that supports a policy.
Procedure
Detailed, step-by-step instructions to perform a task.
COBIT
ISACA's framework for governance and management of enterprise information and technology.
Real-world example

An organization's policy requires quarterly access reviews, but the procedure document has not been updated in four years and staff follow an informal process. The auditor reports the gap and recommends updating the procedure and evidencing each review.

Exam tip: Policies are approved by senior management and state intent; they should not contain technical detail. If a question asks what the auditor reviews first to understand management's intent, pick the policy.

Check yourself

Which document type is optional?

A guideline; policies, standards and procedures are mandatory.

What does COBIT separate governance objectives from?

Management objectives; governance evaluates, directs and monitors, while management plans, builds, runs and monitors.
