ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 1: Information system auditing process

Types of audits, assessments and reviews: IS, compliance, financial, operational, integrated, forensic

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Not every audit asks the same question. Before you plan any work, you need to know what kind of engagement it is, because that decides the objective, the criteria and the evidence you need. The CISA exam often describes an engagement and expects you to recognize its type.

A financial audit gives an opinion on whether financial statements are fairly presented; IS auditors support it by testing the IT controls behind financial systems. A compliance audit tests whether the organization follows specific laws, regulations, contracts or policies, for example a payment card standard. An operational audit evaluates whether a process is efficient and effective. An IS audit evaluates the controls over information systems: general IT controls such as access and change management, and application controls inside a system. An integrated audit combines financial, operational and IS work on one process so the auditor sees the whole control picture.

A forensic audit or investigation is different in purpose: it gathers evidence about suspected fraud or crime in a way that could stand up in court, so evidence handling and chain of custody matter far more. Specialized reviews include administrative audits (efficiency of administrative processes) and third-party or service organization reviews, such as reading a SOC report on a cloud provider.

Assessments and reviews are not always audits. A control self-assessment (CSA) has the process owners evaluate their own controls in facilitated workshops; it builds ownership and awareness but does not replace independent audit. A risk assessment identifies and rates risks rather than testing controls. A review or agreed-upon procedures engagement gives lower, more limited assurance than a full audit. Knowing the level of assurance each gives helps you decide how much you can rely on it.

Key terms

Integrated audit
An audit that combines financial, operational and IS audit work to evaluate all the controls over a process or system together.
Control self-assessment (CSA)
A technique in which process owners and staff evaluate their own controls, usually in facilitated workshops.
Forensic audit
An engagement to collect and analyze evidence about suspected fraud or crime for possible legal or disciplinary use.
Compliance audit
An audit that tests adherence to specific laws, regulations, contracts or internal policies.
Real-world example

A retailer asks internal audit to review its online payment process. The team runs an integrated audit: financial auditors test revenue recognition, while IS auditors test access to the payment application, change management and the interface to the general ledger, and the report gives one combined view.

Exam tip: Control self-assessment does not replace audit. If an answer suggests CSA removes the need for independent testing, it is wrong; CSA supplements audit and improves ownership.

Check yourself

Which audit type needs the strictest evidence handling, and why?

A forensic audit, because its evidence may be used in legal proceedings and must be shown to be unaltered.

What is the main benefit of control self-assessment?

It makes process owners responsible for their controls and raises awareness, which can help detect risks early.
