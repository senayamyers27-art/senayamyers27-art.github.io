ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 1: Information system auditing process

Audit project management: objectives, audit program, fieldwork, resourcing and independence

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Every audit engagement is a small project with a scope, a timeline, staff and deliverables. The current CISA outline expects you to apply project management discipline to audits, so the work finishes on time, stays in scope and produces reliable conclusions.

An engagement usually moves through these phases: planning, where you understand the area, assess risk and set objectives and scope; developing the audit program, which lists the procedures to test each key control; fieldwork, where you perform tests and collect evidence in workpapers; and reporting and follow-up. An engagement letter or memo confirms scope, timing, access and contacts with the auditee. Workpapers record what was done, by whom, what evidence was obtained and what was concluded, so a reviewer can re-trace the reasoning.

Resourcing means making sure the team has the skills the scope requires. If the audit covers a cloud container platform and nobody on the team understands it, the auditor must train, bring in a guest auditor or use an outside expert. When using another expert's work, the auditor must evaluate that expert's competence, independence and the adequacy of their work before relying on it. Supervision and workpaper review by a senior auditor are part of quality.

Independence must be protected throughout. Auditors can advise, but should not make management decisions, design or operate controls, or approve transactions in the area they are auditing. If scope limits are imposed, such as being refused access to a system, the auditor documents it and reports it to audit management and, if needed, the audit committee.

Key terms

Audit program
A step-by-step list of audit procedures designed to meet the objectives of a specific engagement.
Workpapers
The documented record of audit planning, procedures, evidence and conclusions.
Engagement letter
A document that confirms the scope, objectives, timing, responsibilities and access for an audit.
Scope limitation
A restriction on the auditor's access or procedures that prevents gathering sufficient evidence.
Real-world example

During an audit of a trading platform, the business head refuses access to the order database, saying it is too sensitive. The auditor documents the refusal, explains the impact on the audit objective, and escalates to the chief audit executive, who raises it with the audit committee.

Exam tip: If an auditee refuses access or restricts scope, the right answer is to document it and escalate through audit management, not to drop the objective or proceed as if nothing happened.

Check yourself

What must an auditor check before relying on an outside expert's work?

The expert's competence, independence and objectivity, and whether the work is adequate for the audit objective.

What is the purpose of workpapers?

To document the planning, procedures, evidence and conclusions so that the work can be reviewed and the conclusions supported.
