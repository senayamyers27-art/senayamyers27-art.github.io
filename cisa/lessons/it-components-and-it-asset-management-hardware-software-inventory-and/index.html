ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 4: Information systems operations and business resilience

IT components and IT asset management: hardware, software, inventory and licensing

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

IT operations rest on many components: servers, storage, network devices, end-user devices, operating systems, databases, middleware and applications, whether on premises or in the cloud. An auditor does not need to configure them, but must understand what each does and what can go wrong, because risks and controls follow from the technology.

IT asset management (ITAM) keeps track of those components through their life: request, procurement, deployment, maintenance, and retirement with secure disposal. The foundation is an accurate inventory recording each asset's owner, location, configuration, criticality, support status and the data it holds. You cannot patch, monitor, license or protect assets you do not know about, so an incomplete inventory weakens nearly every other control. Automated discovery tools help find devices and software that were never recorded.

Software asset management compares installed software with purchased licenses. Using more copies than licensed creates legal and financial exposure, while buying more than needed wastes money. Controls include restricting who can install software, maintaining a list of approved software, periodic license reconciliation and tracking of cloud subscriptions. End-of-life assets that no longer receive vendor updates are a security concern and should be replaced or isolated.

Disposal is part of the lifecycle. Storage media must be sanitized using methods suitable for the media and data sensitivity, such as cryptographic erase or physical destruction, with evidence recorded. The auditor checks that the inventory is complete by comparing it with discovery scans, purchase records and network data, and samples disposals for sanitization evidence.

Key terms

IT asset management (ITAM)
Tracking and managing IT assets through their lifecycle, from acquisition to disposal.
Software asset management
Managing software licenses and installations to ensure compliance and control costs.
End of life (EOL)
The point after which a vendor no longer supports or updates a product.
Media sanitization
Removing data from storage media so that it cannot be recovered.
Real-world example

An auditor compares the asset inventory with a network discovery scan and finds 40 devices on the network that are not in the inventory, including an old file server running an unsupported operating system that nobody was patching.

Exam tip: An accurate asset inventory is the foundation of IT operations and security. When asked what to do first to manage or protect assets, building or verifying the inventory is often the answer.

Check yourself

Why is an incomplete asset inventory a security risk?

Unknown assets are not patched, monitored or protected, and may be exploited unnoticed.

How can an auditor test inventory completeness?

By comparing it with independent sources such as network discovery scans, purchase records and license data.
