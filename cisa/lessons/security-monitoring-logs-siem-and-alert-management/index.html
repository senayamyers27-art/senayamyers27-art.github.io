ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 5: Protection of information assets

Security monitoring: logs, SIEM and alert management

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Preventive controls eventually fail, so organizations must be able to detect attacks and misuse quickly. Security monitoring collects and analyzes events from across the environment and turns them into alerts that people investigate.

Sources include firewalls, IDS/IPS, endpoint detection and response tools, servers, directories, databases, applications, cloud platforms and physical access systems. A security information and event management (SIEM) system collects logs centrally, normalizes them into a common format, correlates events across sources and applies rules or analytics to raise alerts, such as many failed logins followed by a success from an unusual country. User and entity behavior analytics (UEBA) look for deviations from normal behavior. Security orchestration, automation and response (SOAR) tools automate routine steps such as enriching alerts or isolating a device.

Monitoring only works if it is designed and operated well. Important events must actually be logged and forwarded, clocks synchronized, and logs protected from tampering and kept according to retention requirements. Use cases, meaning the specific threats to detect, should be based on risk. Rules need tuning: too many false positives cause alert fatigue and real attacks get missed. A security operations center (SOC), internal or outsourced, triages alerts according to documented procedures and escalates confirmed incidents to incident response. Metrics such as mean time to detect and respond show performance.

The auditor checks log source coverage against the asset inventory, reviews use cases and tuning, samples alerts to see whether they were investigated and closed appropriately, and checks that privileged activity, including that of security staff, is monitored.

Key terms

SIEM
Security information and event management: a system that collects, correlates and analyzes security events and raises alerts.
Correlation rule
Logic that links related events from different sources to identify suspicious patterns.
Alert fatigue
Desensitization caused by too many alerts, especially false positives, leading to missed real threats.
Security operations center (SOC)
A team that monitors, triages and responds to security alerts.
Real-world example

An auditor compares the SIEM's log sources with the asset inventory and finds that the payroll database and two domain controllers send no logs. She also samples 20 high-severity alerts and finds five closed without investigation notes.

Exam tip: A SIEM with missing log sources or unreviewed alerts gives false comfort. Coverage, tuning and evidence that alerts are investigated are what make monitoring effective.

Check yourself

What does correlation add over reviewing single logs?

It links events across sources to reveal patterns, such as an attack moving between systems, that no single log shows.

How can an auditor test whether monitoring is working?

By checking log source coverage, reviewing use cases and sampling alerts for timely, documented investigation.
