ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 2: Governance and management of IT

Laws, regulations and industry standards affecting the organization

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Organizations operate under a web of legal and contractual obligations: privacy and data protection laws, financial reporting rules, sector regulations for health or banking, breach notification laws, and industry standards such as the Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard. An IS auditor must understand which of these apply, because they become the criteria against which controls are judged.

The auditor does not need to be a lawyer, but should know how the organization identifies its obligations. A mature organization keeps a register of applicable laws, regulations and contractual requirements, assigns owners, assesses the gap between requirements and current controls, and monitors for changes. Legal counsel and compliance functions usually own this process; IT translates requirements into technical controls such as encryption, retention and logging.

When a new requirement appears, the first step is for management to assess its applicability and impact, then plan and implement controls. Specific measures, such as buying tools or changing contracts, should follow that assessment rather than precede it. Requirements can conflict across countries, for example one law requiring records to be kept for years and another requiring personal data to be deleted when no longer needed. The organization must resolve these conflicts with legal advice and document its decisions.

Industry standards and frameworks, such as ISO/IEC 27001 for information security management or NIST publications, are usually voluntary unless a law or contract makes them mandatory. They are still useful as criteria for audits and as a way to structure controls. For the exam, remember that contracts with customers or card brands can impose obligations as binding in practice as law.

Key terms

Regulatory compliance
Meeting the requirements imposed by laws and regulations that apply to the organization.
Compliance register
A maintained list of applicable legal, regulatory and contractual requirements with owners and status.
Breach notification
A legal requirement to inform regulators or affected people when certain data is compromised.
Industry standard
A requirement set developed by an industry body, such as for card payments, which may become binding through contracts.
Real-world example

A software company expands into a new country with a strict data protection law. Before any technical changes, the compliance team maps the law's requirements against current practices, finds gaps in consent records and data transfer terms, and assigns remediation owners with deadlines.

Exam tip: The first audit question about a new law is whether management has identified and assessed the requirements. Controls come after that assessment.

Check yourself

Who usually owns identifying applicable laws: IT, legal/compliance or audit?

Legal and compliance functions, with IT implementing technical controls and audit providing assurance.

Can a voluntary standard become mandatory?

Yes, when a law or contract requires it, such as a payment card standard required by agreements with card brands.
