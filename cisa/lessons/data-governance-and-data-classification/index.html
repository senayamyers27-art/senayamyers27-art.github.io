Data governance and data classification

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Data governance defines who is accountable for data, how its quality and protection are managed, and how decisions about it are made. Without it, nobody owns data quality, sensitive records spread across uncontrolled copies, and controls are applied inconsistently.

Key roles recur on the exam. The data owner is a business manager accountable for a set of data; they decide its classification, approve who may access it and set requirements for retention. The data custodian, often IT, implements and operates the controls the owner specifies, such as backups and access settings. Data stewards look after data quality and definitions day to day. Users access data according to their authorized role. Many organizations also have a data governance council that sets policy and resolves disputes.

Data classification assigns data to categories, for example public, internal, confidential and restricted, based on its sensitivity and value. Each level has handling rules for storage, transmission, sharing, labeling and disposal. Classification lets protection be proportionate: expensive controls go where they matter most. It also underpins data loss prevention rules, encryption requirements and access decisions. Classification should be reviewed over time, because data can become more or less sensitive.

Data quality is part of governance too. Accurate, complete, consistent and timely data supports reliable decisions and reports. Auditors review whether owners are assigned, whether a data inventory exists, whether classification is applied and matched by controls, and whether quality issues are measured and fixed.

Key terms

Data owner
The business manager accountable for a data set, who decides its classification and approves access.
Data custodian
The party, often IT, that implements and maintains controls over data on the owner's behalf.
Data classification
Assigning data to sensitivity levels that determine how it must be handled and protected.
Data steward
A person responsible for the quality, definitions and proper use of data day to day.
Real-world example

An auditor finds that a customer database has no named owner, so the DBA grants access to anyone who asks. She recommends assigning the head of customer service as owner, classifying the data as confidential and requiring the owner's approval for all access.

Exam tip: The owner decides and is accountable; the custodian implements. When asked who classifies data or approves access, choose the business owner, not IT or security.

Check yourself

Who approves access to a data set?

The data owner.

Why classify data?

So protection and handling are proportionate to the data's sensitivity and value, and so controls are applied consistently.
