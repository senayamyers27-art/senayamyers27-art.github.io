ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 2: Governance and management of IT

IT resource management: segregation of duties, staffing, budgeting and portfolio management

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

IT resources include people, money, infrastructure and applications. Managing them well means having the right skills, spending wisely, choosing the right investments and organizing duties so no single person can cause and hide a serious error or fraud.

Segregation of duties (SoD) separates incompatible functions: requesting, approving, recording and custody of assets, or in IT terms developing code, approving changes and moving code to production. Other conflicts include security administration combined with system administration, or a database administrator also approving their own access. Where full SoD is not possible, such as in a small team, compensating controls like independent log review, supervisory approval and reconciliations reduce the risk. Auditors often build an SoD matrix to find conflicts.

People controls include background checks during hiring, clear job descriptions, training, mandatory vacations and job rotation (which can uncover fraud), succession planning to avoid key-person dependency, and prompt removal of access on termination. When contractors or temporary staff are used, the same controls must apply.

Financial and portfolio management ensure IT spending delivers value. IT budgets should link to the strategic plan, and actual spending should be tracked against budget. Portfolio management evaluates all proposed and running investments together, ranking them by value, risk and alignment and deciding which to fund, continue or stop. Chargeback or showback models help business units see the cost of IT they consume. Auditors check whether investment decisions are made on documented business cases and reviewed over time.

Key terms

Segregation of duties
Dividing incompatible duties among different people so that no single person can commit and conceal errors or fraud.
Compensating control
An alternative control that reduces risk when the ideal control, such as full segregation of duties, cannot be applied.
Portfolio management
Managing a set of IT investments together to maximize value and alignment within available resources.
Job rotation
Periodically moving staff between roles, which reduces dependence on individuals and can reveal irregularities.
Real-world example

In a five-person IT team, one engineer both writes code and deploys it. The auditor recommends that the IT manager review a weekly report of all production changes against approved tickets, and that deployment logs be sent to a system the engineer cannot edit.

Exam tip: The classic IT SoD conflict is the same person developing and moving code to production. If full separation is impossible, look for a compensating detective control done by someone independent.

Check yourself

Give two examples of incompatible IT duties.

Developing code and migrating it to production; administering security and approving one's own access; entering transactions and approving them.

Why do mandatory vacations help detect fraud?

Someone else performs the duties while the employee is away and may notice irregularities that required ongoing concealment.
