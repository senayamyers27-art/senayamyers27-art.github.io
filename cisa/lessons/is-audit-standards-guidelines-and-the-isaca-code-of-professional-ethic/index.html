ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 1: Information system auditing process

IS audit standards, guidelines and the ISACA Code of Professional Ethics

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

An IS auditor gives other people confidence that information systems are controlled. That confidence is only worth something if the audit is done to a recognized standard by someone who is honest and independent. ISACA publishes the IT Audit Framework (ITAF), which contains mandatory standards, guidelines that explain how to apply them, and tools and techniques. The CISA exam expects you to think like someone who follows these standards by default.

The standards fall into three groups. General standards cover the audit charter, organizational independence, auditor objectivity, reasonable expectation that the audit can be completed, due professional care, proficiency and assertions. Performance standards cover risk assessment and planning, supervision, materiality, evidence, using the work of other experts, and irregularities. Reporting standards cover the report itself and follow-up activities. Guidelines are not mandatory, but an auditor who departs from them should be able to justify why.

Independence has two parts. Organizational independence means the audit function reports to a level, usually the audit committee of the board, that lets it work without interference. Independence in fact and appearance means the individual auditor has no conflicting interest or role. Typical threats are self-review (auditing your own work), management participation (making decisions that belong to management) and familiarity. When independence is impaired, the auditor discloses it to the appropriate parties rather than quietly carrying on.

The ISACA Code of Professional Ethics asks members to support standards and controls, perform duties objectively and with due care, serve stakeholders lawfully, keep information confidential unless disclosure is legally required, stay competent, inform the appropriate parties of the results of work, and support education of the public. Breaking the code can lead to disciplinary action, including losing the certification. On the exam, the ethical answer is usually the one that reports honestly through the proper channel.

Key terms

ITAF
ISACA's IT Audit Framework: mandatory standards, supporting guidelines, and tools and techniques for IS audit and assurance work.
Independence in appearance
The absence of circumstances that would lead a reasonable third party to doubt the auditor's objectivity.
Due professional care
Applying the skill and diligence that a prudent, competent auditor would use in the same circumstances.
Self-review threat
The risk that an auditor will not critically evaluate work they performed or designed themselves.
Real-world example

A newly hired auditor previously configured the company's identity management system as a consultant. When assigned to audit it, she tells the chief audit executive about her earlier role, and the audit is reassigned to a colleague while she audits a different area.

Exam tip: When a question involves an independence problem, the answer is almost always to disclose it to audit management or the audit committee, not to carry on or to narrow the scope quietly.

Check yourself

Are ITAF guidelines mandatory?

No. The standards are mandatory; guidelines explain how to apply them, and departures from guidelines should be justified.

What should an auditor do when independence is impaired?

Disclose the impairment to the appropriate parties, such as audit management, and let them decide whether to reassign the work.
