Systems availability and capacity management

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Users expect systems to be there when needed and to respond quickly. Availability management plans and monitors the ability of IT services to perform when required; capacity management makes sure resources such as processing, memory, storage and network bandwidth meet current and future demand at acceptable cost.

Availability is shaped by reliability (how rarely components fail), maintainability (how quickly they can be fixed) and resilience (how well the service continues when parts fail). Common measures include availability percentage, mean time between failures (MTBF) and mean time to repair (MTTR). Techniques to improve availability include redundant components, clustering, load balancing, failover, proactive maintenance and monitoring that alerts before users notice problems.

Capacity management relies on monitoring current utilization, analyzing trends and forecasting demand from business plans, such as a new product launch or seasonal peaks. When forecasts show resources will run out, management plans upgrades, tuning or scaling before performance suffers. In cloud environments, auto-scaling can add capacity automatically, but it needs limits and cost monitoring. Performance tuning and workload scheduling can also make better use of existing capacity.

Auditors check whether availability and capacity requirements are defined, often in service level agreements; whether monitoring covers critical systems; whether trends are reviewed and acted on; and whether incidents caused by capacity shortages are analyzed. A common finding is monitoring that exists but whose alerts nobody reviews, or growth that is noticed only when a disk fills and a service stops.

Key terms

Availability
The proportion of agreed time that a service is usable, often expressed as a percentage.
Capacity management
Ensuring IT resources meet current and future demand cost-effectively.
Mean time between failures (MTBF)
The average operating time between failures of a component or system.
Mean time to repair (MTTR)
The average time needed to restore a failed component or service.
Real-world example

Storage on an order database grows 8% a month with 25% free. Capacity reports forecast it will fill in about three months, so the infrastructure team schedules an expansion for next month's maintenance window instead of waiting for an outage.

Exam tip: Capacity management is proactive: it uses trends and business forecasts to act before resources run out. Waiting for a failure is the wrong answer.

Check yourself

What data does capacity planning rely on?

Current utilization, historical trends and forecasts of business demand.

Name two techniques that improve availability.

Any two of: redundancy, clustering, load balancing, failover, proactive maintenance and monitoring.
