ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 5: Protection of information assets

Identity and access management: authentication, authorization, provisioning and access reviews

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Identity and access management (IAM) ensures that the right people and systems have the right access to the right resources, and no more. Access control failures are among the most common audit findings, so this topic is heavily tested.

Identification is claiming an identity, such as a user ID. Authentication proves it using factors: something you know (password), something you have (token, phone, security key) or something you are (biometric). Multifactor authentication combines different types; two passwords are still one factor type. Authorization decides what an authenticated identity may do, based on principles such as least privilege and need to know. Models include role-based access control, where permissions attach to job roles, and attribute-based access control, where rules consider attributes such as department or location. Accountability comes from unique IDs and logging, which is why shared accounts are a finding.

Provisioning follows the joiner, mover, leaver cycle. Joiners receive access based on an approved request, ideally through predefined roles. Movers should lose old access when they gain new, avoiding privilege creep. Leavers must be disabled promptly, best done by linking HR termination events to automated deprovisioning. Privileged accounts, such as administrators, need extra controls: separate admin accounts, multifactor authentication, just-in-time elevation, session recording and regular review. Single sign-on and federation using protocols such as SAML or OpenID Connect reduce password sprawl but make the identity provider critical.

Periodic access reviews, or recertifications, have data or application owners confirm that each user's access is still appropriate. The auditor tests by comparing HR leaver lists to active accounts, sampling new and changed access for approval, reviewing privileged accounts and checking that review results are acted on.

Key terms

Least privilege
Granting users only the minimum access needed to perform their duties.
Multifactor authentication (MFA)
Authentication that combines two or more different factor types.
Privilege creep
Gradual accumulation of access rights as users change roles without removing old access.
Access recertification
A periodic review in which owners confirm that users' access remains appropriate.
Real-world example

Comparing the HR leaver list with directory accounts, an auditor finds 14 former staff still enabled, two of whom logged in after leaving. The root cause is a manual email-based leaver process, so she recommends automated deprovisioning triggered by HR and monthly reconciliation.

Exam tip: Owners review access; administrators implement. For leavers, the best control links HR terminations to automatic account removal, supported by periodic reviews.

Check yourself

Is a password plus a PIN multifactor authentication?

No, both are something you know, so it is a single factor type.

What causes privilege creep and how is it controlled?

Users keep old access when changing roles; it is controlled by removing access on transfer and by periodic access reviews.
