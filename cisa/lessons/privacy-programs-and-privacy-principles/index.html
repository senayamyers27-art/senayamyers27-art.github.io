Privacy programs and privacy principles

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Privacy is about the proper handling of personal information: how it is collected, used, shared, retained and disposed of, and what rights individuals have over it. Security protects data; privacy decides what the organization should do with it in the first place. A privacy program brings these decisions under governance.

Widely accepted privacy principles, reflected in laws and frameworks around the world, include: lawful basis and transparency (people are told what is collected and why); purpose limitation (data is used only for the purposes stated); data minimization (collect only what is needed); accuracy; storage limitation (keep it no longer than necessary); integrity and confidentiality (protect it); individual rights such as access, correction and deletion; and accountability (the organization can demonstrate compliance).

A privacy program typically has an accountable leader such as a privacy officer or data protection officer, a privacy policy and notices, an inventory of personal data and where it flows, privacy impact assessments for new systems and changes, procedures for responding to individuals' requests, contracts that bind processors, breach response procedures and training. Privacy by design means building these considerations into systems from the start rather than adding them later.

Auditors review whether the program exists and works: is there a data inventory, are impact assessments done before launch, are requests handled on time, are retention schedules applied, and are third parties bound by appropriate terms? Reusing data for a new purpose, collecting more than needed, and keeping data forever are common findings.

Key terms

Purpose limitation
The principle that personal data is used only for the specific purposes for which it was collected.
Data minimization
Collecting and keeping only the personal data that is necessary for the stated purpose.
Privacy impact assessment (PIA)
An evaluation of how a new system or change affects personal data and privacy risk, done before it is implemented.
Privacy by design
Building privacy protections into systems and processes from the start.
Real-world example

A marketing team wants to analyze purchase histories to target ads. The privacy officer runs an impact assessment, finds that customers were told data would be used only for fulfilment, and requires consent before the new use begins.

Exam tip: Security and privacy overlap but differ. A question about using data for a new purpose or collecting too much is about privacy principles, not encryption.

Check yourself

What is the purpose of a privacy impact assessment?

To identify and address privacy risks of a new system or change before it goes live.

Which principle limits how long personal data is kept?

Storage limitation (retention only as long as necessary).
