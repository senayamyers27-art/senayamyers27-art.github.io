ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 5: Protection of information assets

Cloud, virtualized, mobile, wireless and IoT environments

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Modern IT runs on shared and distributed platforms: virtual machines, containers, cloud services, phones, wireless networks and connected devices. Each brings specific risks the auditor must understand.

Virtualization runs many virtual machines on one physical host through a hypervisor. It improves efficiency but concentrates risk: a hypervisor compromise affects every guest, and virtual machines can be created quickly without proper controls. Controls include hardening and patching hypervisors, restricting management consoles, separating workloads of different sensitivity and managing images and templates. Containers package applications with their dependencies and need secure images, registries and orchestration.

Cloud services follow a shared responsibility model. In infrastructure as a service (IaaS), the provider secures the physical infrastructure and hypervisor while the customer secures operating systems, applications, identities and data. In platform as a service (PaaS) the provider also manages the platform, and in software as a service (SaaS) the provider runs the application while the customer manages users, access, configuration and data. In every model the customer stays accountable for its data. Common cloud findings are misconfigured storage, excessive permissions and missing logs. Assurance comes from contracts, SOC reports and certifications, and the customer's own configuration monitoring.

Mobile devices are easily lost and mix personal and business use; mobile device management (MDM) enforces screen locks, encryption, app controls and remote wipe, often through a separate work container. Wireless networks should use current protections such as WPA3 or WPA2-Enterprise with strong authentication, and guest networks should be separated. Internet of Things (IoT) devices often have weak default credentials, limited patching and long lifespans, so they should be inventoried, placed in isolated network segments and monitored.

Key terms

Shared responsibility model
The division of security duties between a cloud provider and its customer, which varies by service model.
Hypervisor
Software that creates and runs virtual machines on a physical host.
Mobile device management (MDM)
Tools that enforce security policies on mobile devices, including encryption and remote wipe.
Internet of Things (IoT)
Network-connected physical devices such as sensors, cameras and controllers.
Real-world example

An auditor reviewing a cloud account finds a storage bucket holding customer exports configured for public access. The provider's controls are fine; the misconfiguration is the customer's responsibility, so she recommends configuration monitoring that alerts on public storage.

Exam tip: In the cloud, the customer always keeps accountability for its data, identities and configuration. Most cloud breaches come from customer misconfiguration, not provider failure.

Check yourself

In SaaS, who manages user access to the application?

The customer.

Why isolate IoT devices on their own network segment?

They are often poorly secured and hard to patch, so isolation limits what an attacker can reach through them.
