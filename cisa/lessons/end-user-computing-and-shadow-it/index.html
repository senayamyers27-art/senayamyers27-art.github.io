End-user computing and shadow IT

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

End-user computing (EUC) means applications built or managed by business users rather than IT: spreadsheets with complex formulas, desktop databases, reporting tools and low-code apps. EUC is valuable because it is flexible and fast, but it often supports important decisions and financial reports without the controls applied to formal systems.

Typical EUC risks include formula errors, no version control, no testing, no documentation, one person who understands the tool, weak access control, no backup and data copied out of controlled systems. A single wrong cell reference in a spreadsheet used for pricing or financial reporting can cause material errors. A good EUC program inventories critical EUC tools, rates their risk based on use and complexity, and applies proportionate controls: locked formulas, access restriction, version control, independent review of changes, reconciliation to source data and backups.

Shadow IT is technology acquired or used without the IT function's knowledge or approval, most often cloud software subscribed to with a credit card. It can introduce unvetted vendors, unknown data locations, weak authentication, licensing issues and data that is not backed up or covered by retention rules. It usually appears because users have needs IT is not meeting quickly enough.

The best first step is discovery: finding what is in use, for example through expense reports, network traffic analysis or a cloud access security broker, and then assessing risk. Useful services can be brought under governance, risky ones replaced, and policies and approval processes made fast enough that people use them. Blanket bans usually push shadow IT further out of sight.

Key terms

End-user computing (EUC)
Applications and tools developed or managed by business users outside the formal IT function.
Shadow IT
Technology used in the organization without IT's knowledge or approval.
EUC inventory
A list of end-user tools with their owners, purpose and risk rating.
Cloud access security broker (CASB)
A tool that discovers and controls use of cloud services.
Real-world example

Finance calculates quarterly revenue in a spreadsheet with 30 linked tabs maintained by one analyst. The auditor finds no version history or review. The team adds the file to the EUC inventory as high risk, locks formulas, keeps versions and requires a second person to review changes each quarter.

Exam tip: For shadow IT, the best first step is discovery and risk assessment, not blocking everything. For EUC, the key risks are lack of change control, testing, access control and backup.

Check yourself

Why is shadow IT hard to govern?

IT does not know it exists, so it is not assessed, secured, backed up or covered by contracts and policies.

What controls suit a high-risk spreadsheet?

Inventory and ownership, access restriction, locked formulas, version control, independent review of changes, reconciliation and backup.
