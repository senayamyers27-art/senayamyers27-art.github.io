ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 5: Protection of information assets

Physical and environmental controls

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Logical controls are useless if someone can walk out with a server or if a flood destroys the data center. Physical and environmental controls protect facilities, equipment and the people who work there.

Physical access controls work in layers: site perimeter such as fences and lighting, building entrances with guards or receptionists, and internal secure areas such as data centers and wiring closets. Access methods include badges, PINs, biometrics and locks, and access should be granted by need and reviewed regularly. Mantraps, or access control vestibules, allow one person through at a time and stop tailgating. Visitors should be registered, badged and escorted. CCTV and alarms detect and record events, and access logs support investigations. Data centers should have minimal signage that reveals their purpose.

Environmental controls protect equipment from power, temperature, humidity, water and fire. Power protection includes uninterruptible power supplies for short outages and conditioning, plus generators for longer ones, and both need regular testing. Heating, ventilation and air conditioning keep temperature and humidity within range. Water detection sensors under raised floors warn of leaks. Fire protection combines detection (smoke and heat detectors) with suppression. Wet-pipe sprinklers hold water in the pipes; dry-pipe and pre-action systems keep pipes dry until triggered, reducing accidental discharge, and pre-action is common in staffed data centers. Gas-based clean agent systems suppress fire without water but need safety procedures; carbon dioxide is dangerous in occupied rooms.

Equipment should be located away from hazards, and media and equipment leaving the site should be authorized and protected. The auditor inspects facilities, reviews access lists and logs, and checks maintenance and test records for UPS, generators and suppression systems.

Key terms

Mantrap
An entry area with two doors that lets only one authenticated person pass at a time, preventing tailgating.
Tailgating
Following an authorized person through a secured door without authenticating.
Pre-action sprinkler
A sprinkler system whose pipes stay dry until a detector triggers, reducing accidental water damage.
Uninterruptible power supply (UPS)
A battery-based device that provides short-term power and conditioning during outages.
Real-world example

During a data center walkthrough, the auditor sees staff hold the door for colleagues and finds that 12 people on the access list left the company last year. She recommends a mantrap, a quarterly access list review by the facilities owner and awareness training on tailgating.

Exam tip: A camera or sign-in sheet detects or deters; a mantrap prevents tailgating. For staffed data centers, pre-action sprinklers balance safety and water damage risk.

Check yourself

Why test generators and UPS units regularly?

To confirm they will actually work and carry the load during a real power failure.

Why is carbon dioxide suppression unsuitable for occupied rooms?

It displaces oxygen and can harm or kill people in the room.
