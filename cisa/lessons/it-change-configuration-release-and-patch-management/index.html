ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 4: Information systems operations and business resilience

IT change, configuration, release and patch management

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Changes are one of the most common causes of outages and control failures. Change management ensures that changes to production systems are requested, assessed, approved, tested, implemented and reviewed in a controlled way, and it is one of the IT general controls auditors test most often.

A typical change process records a request, assesses impact and risk, obtains approval from appropriate owners or a change advisory board, tests the change in a non-production environment, schedules and implements it with a rollback plan, and reviews it afterward. Standard changes are low-risk, pre-approved and repeatable. Emergency changes can bypass some steps to fix urgent issues, but must still be logged and approved retrospectively. Segregation of duties is central: the person who develops a change should not be the one who approves it or moves it into production.

Configuration management maintains accurate records of configuration items and their relationships, often in a configuration management database, so the impact of a change can be judged and unauthorized configuration drift can be detected. Release management bundles approved changes and deploys them in an orderly way. Patch management keeps systems updated against known vulnerabilities: it tracks vendor releases, assesses urgency, tests patches, deploys them within defined timeframes based on risk, and verifies installation.

To test change management, auditors sample changes from system logs or version control and trace them to approved tickets, checking for testing evidence, approval before implementation and separation between developer and implementer. Sampling from the system, not only from the ticket list, is what reveals unauthorized changes.

Key terms

Change advisory board (CAB)
A group that assesses and approves or rejects significant changes.
Emergency change
An urgent change implemented quickly, then documented and approved after the fact.
Configuration item
Any component that must be managed to deliver an IT service, recorded with its attributes and relationships.
Patch management
Identifying, testing and deploying software updates that fix vulnerabilities or defects.
Real-world example

An auditor extracts all deployments to production from the pipeline logs for a quarter and traces 25 to change tickets. Three have no ticket at all and one was approved by the same developer who wrote the code, so she reports both unauthorized changes and a segregation-of-duties weakness.

Exam tip: To find unauthorized changes, sample from what actually changed in production (system logs, version control) and trace back to approvals. Sampling only from approved tickets cannot reveal changes that never had one.

Check yourself

What must happen after an emergency change?

It must be documented, reviewed and approved retrospectively.

Why test patches before deploying them?

To make sure they do not break applications or cause outages in production.
