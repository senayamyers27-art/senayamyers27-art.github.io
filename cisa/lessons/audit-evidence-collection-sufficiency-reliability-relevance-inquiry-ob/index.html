ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 1: Information system auditing process

Audit evidence collection: sufficiency, reliability, relevance; inquiry, observation, inspection, reperformance

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Audit conclusions are only as good as the evidence behind them. ISACA's standards require evidence that is sufficient, reliable and relevant to support the auditor's opinion, and many exam questions ask which evidence is best.

Sufficient means there is enough of it, for example an adequate sample size. Relevant means it actually addresses the audit objective; evidence that a password policy exists is not relevant to whether it is enforced. Reliable depends on the source and method. As a rule, evidence from an independent outside source is more reliable than evidence from the auditee; evidence the auditor obtains directly is more reliable than evidence handed over; documentary evidence beats oral statements; and original documents beat copies. Evidence produced by a system with strong controls is more reliable than evidence from a weakly controlled system.

The main collection techniques each have strengths. Inquiry, meaning interviews and questionnaires, helps understanding but is weak alone and needs corroboration. Observation shows a process at one moment but people may behave differently when watched. Inspection examines documents, configurations and records. Reperformance means the auditor independently executes the control or calculation, which gives strong evidence. Walkthroughs trace one transaction end to end to confirm understanding of the design. Computer-assisted techniques extract and analyze data directly.

All evidence must be documented in workpapers with source, date and how it was obtained, and protected from alteration. When electronic evidence is involved, the auditor should obtain it directly from the system where possible, and consider hashing or read-only copies to show it was not changed.

Key terms

Sufficient evidence
Enough evidence, in quantity, to support the audit conclusion.
Reperformance
The auditor independently executing a control or calculation to confirm it produces the correct result.
Corroboration
Obtaining additional evidence to confirm information gathered through inquiry or other weaker sources.
Walkthrough
Tracing a single transaction through a process to confirm the auditor's understanding of controls.
Real-world example

A system administrator says all servers enforce 12-character passwords. The auditor does not accept this statement alone. She extracts the password policy settings directly from the directory service while the administrator watches and confirms the value is 12 on every domain.

Exam tip: When ranking evidence, prefer independent over internal, auditor-obtained over auditee-provided, and documentary or reperformed over oral statements.

Check yourself

Why is inquiry alone usually not enough?

Oral statements may be inaccurate or self-serving; they must be corroborated by inspection, observation or reperformance.

Which is more reliable: a bank confirmation received directly by the auditor or a bank statement supplied by the client?

The confirmation received directly from the bank, because it comes from an independent source without passing through the auditee.
