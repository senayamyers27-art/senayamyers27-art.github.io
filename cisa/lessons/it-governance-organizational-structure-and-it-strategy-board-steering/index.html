ISACA Certified Information Systems Auditor (CISA) 2024 job practice · Domain 2: Governance and management of IT

IT governance, organizational structure and IT strategy: board, steering committee, business alignment

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

IT governance is how an organization makes sure its use of technology supports its goals, delivers value, manages risk and uses resources responsibly. Governance is different from management. Governance sets direction, makes high-level decisions and monitors performance; management plans, builds, runs and monitors activities within that direction.

The board of directors is ultimately accountable for governance, including IT governance. Boards often use committees: an audit committee oversees internal controls and audit, and a board-level IT strategy committee may advise on technology direction. At management level, an IT steering committee of senior business and IT leaders prioritizes projects, approves investments, monitors progress and resolves conflicts over resources. The chief information officer leads IT, and in many organizations a chief information security officer leads security, ideally with enough independence to raise risks without being overruled by IT delivery pressure.

IT strategy should flow from business strategy. A good strategic plan states where the business is going, what IT must deliver to support it, which investments come first and how success will be measured. Short-term operational plans then implement the strategy. Signs of poor alignment include projects approved only by IT, unclear business sponsors, and IT measuring itself only on technical metrics.

Organizational structure matters for control. Reporting lines should avoid conflicts, for example security should not report to the person whose systems it must challenge if that silences concerns. Audit should report functionally to the audit committee. The auditor reviews governance by examining committee charters, minutes, strategy documents and how decisions are actually made.

Key terms

IT governance
The leadership, structures and processes that ensure IT supports the organization's strategy and objectives.
IT steering committee
A management committee of business and IT leaders that prioritizes, approves and monitors IT investments.
Business alignment
The degree to which IT plans and investments support the organization's strategic goals.
IT strategy committee
A board-level committee that advises the board on the strategic direction of IT.
Real-world example

An auditor reviews steering committee minutes for a year and finds that business leaders attended only one of twelve meetings. Projects were effectively chosen by IT alone. She reports a governance weakness: investments may not reflect business priorities.

Exam tip: Governance is the board's job; management executes. If an answer places ultimate accountability with the CIO or IT department, look for the board instead.

Check yourself

What is the difference between governance and management?

Governance sets direction and monitors; management plans, builds and runs activities within that direction.

What evidence shows whether a steering committee is effective?

Charters, attendance by business leaders, minutes showing real decisions on priorities and investment, and follow-up on project performance.
