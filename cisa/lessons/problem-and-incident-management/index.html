Problem and incident management

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

When something goes wrong in IT operations, two related but different processes handle it. Incident management restores normal service as quickly as possible. Problem management finds and removes the underlying cause so the same incidents do not keep happening. The exam regularly tests the difference.

An incident is any unplanned interruption or reduction in the quality of a service. The incident process includes detection and logging, classification and prioritization by impact and urgency, initial diagnosis, escalation to specialist teams or management when needed, resolution (often with a workaround), and closure after confirming with the user. Every incident should be recorded, even if fixed quickly, so trends can be analyzed. Service desk staff follow defined escalation rules so serious incidents reach the right people quickly.

A problem is the unknown cause of one or more incidents. Problem management analyzes incident trends, performs root cause analysis using techniques such as the five whys or fishbone diagrams, documents known errors and workarounds in a known error database, and raises changes to fix the cause permanently. Problem management can be reactive, after incidents occur, or proactive, spotting weaknesses before they cause incidents.

Security incidents follow a specialized response process, but the general incident process should recognize when an incident might be security-related and hand it over. The auditor checks that incidents are logged completely, prioritized consistently, resolved within agreed times, and that recurring incidents lead to problem records and permanent fixes.

Key terms

Incident
An unplanned interruption or reduction in the quality of an IT service.
Problem
The underlying, often unknown, cause of one or more incidents.
Root cause analysis
A structured investigation to identify the fundamental cause of a problem.
Known error
A problem whose root cause has been identified and for which a workaround is documented.
Real-world example

A web portal crashes every Monday morning and the service desk restarts it each time. Problem management reviews the pattern, traces it to a weekend batch job that fills a log partition, and raises a change to rotate logs, ending the crashes.

Exam tip: Incident management restores service fast, even with a workaround. Problem management finds and fixes the root cause. Repeated identical incidents point to missing problem management.

Check yourself

What is the main goal of incident management?

To restore normal service as quickly as possible and minimize business impact.

What should happen when the same incident recurs many times?

A problem record should be opened and root cause analysis performed so the cause can be fixed permanently.
