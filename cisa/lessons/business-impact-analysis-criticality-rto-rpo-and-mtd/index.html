Business impact analysis: criticality, RTO, RPO and MTD

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 29, 2026 · Leer en español

Business resilience starts by understanding what the organization cannot do without. The business impact analysis (BIA) identifies critical business processes, the resources they depend on and the impact of losing them over time. Every later decision about continuity and recovery depends on its results.

A BIA typically gathers information through interviews, questionnaires and workshops with process owners. For each process it identifies dependencies such as applications, data, people, facilities and suppliers; estimates financial, operational, legal, regulatory and reputational impact over time; and ranks processes by criticality. Senior management should review and approve the results, since they reflect business priorities rather than IT preferences.

The BIA sets key recovery measures. The maximum tolerable downtime (MTD), also called maximum tolerable period of disruption, is the longest a process can be unavailable before the damage becomes unacceptable. The recovery time objective (RTO) is the target time to restore a system or process after disruption, and must be shorter than the MTD. The recovery point objective (RPO) is the maximum acceptable data loss, measured in time back from the incident, and it drives how often data must be backed up or replicated. The service delivery objective describes the reduced service level acceptable during recovery.

Low RTOs and RPOs are expensive, requiring hot sites, replication and redundancy, while higher values allow cheaper options. The BIA helps balance cost against impact. It should be updated when the business or systems change significantly.

Key terms

Business impact analysis (BIA)
An analysis of critical processes, their dependencies and the impact of disruption over time.
Recovery time objective (RTO)
The target time within which a system or process must be restored after a disruption.
Recovery point objective (RPO)
The maximum acceptable amount of data loss, measured in time before the disruption.
Maximum tolerable downtime (MTD)
The longest time a process can be disrupted before the impact becomes unacceptable.
Real-world example

An online retailer's BIA finds that order processing loses significant revenue after four hours and cannot lose more than 15 minutes of orders. It sets an RTO of two hours and an RPO of 15 minutes, which leads to database replication to a second site rather than nightly backups alone.

Exam tip: RPO is about data loss and drives backup frequency; RTO is about time to restore and drives recovery site choice. The BIA comes first, before choosing strategies or sites.

Check yourself

Which measure determines how often data must be backed up?

The recovery point objective (RPO).

What is the relationship between RTO and MTD?

The RTO must be shorter than the MTD so recovery happens before the damage becomes unacceptable.
