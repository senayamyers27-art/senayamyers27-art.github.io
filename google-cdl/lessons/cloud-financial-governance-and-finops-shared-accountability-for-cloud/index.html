Cloud financial governance and FinOps: shared accountability for cloud cost

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

In a traditional data center, spending is controlled up front: finance approves a hardware purchase, and afterwards the cost is fixed. In the cloud, any engineer with permission can create resources in minutes, and costs accumulate by the second. That flexibility is valuable, but without new practices it can lead to surprise bills, idle resources and no clear idea of who spent what.

Cloud financial governance is the set of policies, processes and tools that keep cloud spending aligned with business value. FinOps, short for cloud financial operations, is the widely used practice for doing this. It brings together finance, technology and business teams so that everyone shares responsibility for cost. Engineers see the cost of their decisions, finance understands the variable nature of cloud spending, and business leaders decide trade-offs between speed, quality and cost.

FinOps is often described as a cycle of three phases. Inform: make costs visible and allocate them to teams, products and projects, using the resource hierarchy, labels, billing reports and dashboards. Optimize: reduce waste and get better rates, for example by removing idle resources, rightsizing over-provisioned VMs, using autoscaling, choosing appropriate storage classes and buying commitments for steady workloads. Operate: set budgets and targets, automate controls and continuously improve.

A key idea is that the goal is not simply to spend less but to get the most value per dollar. Spending more on a service that brings in more revenue can be a good decision; paying for forgotten test VMs is not.

Google Cloud supports FinOps with tools covered in the following lessons: the resource hierarchy for organizing projects, Cloud Billing reports and exports, budgets and alerts, quotas, labels, and recommendations from Active Assist (the Recommender) that identify idle resources and rightsizing opportunities. Organizations often set up a central team, sometimes within the cloud center of excellence, to drive these practices.

Key terms

FinOps
A practice that brings finance, technology and business together to manage cloud costs and maximize value.
Cost allocation
Assigning cloud costs to the teams, products or projects that incur them.
Rightsizing
Changing resources to the size that actually matches their workload.
Active Assist recommendations
Google Cloud suggestions, such as removing idle VMs or resizing machines, to reduce cost and improve security.
Real-world example

A software company's cloud bill doubles in a quarter and nobody knows why. It forms a FinOps group, labels every project by team, gives each team a cost dashboard and budget, and acts on recommendations to delete idle VMs. Waste falls, and teams start considering cost when designing features.

Exam tip: FinOps is about shared accountability and maximizing business value, not only cutting cost. Look for answers that involve visibility and collaboration between finance and engineering.

Check yourself

What are the three FinOps phases?

Inform (visibility and allocation), optimize (reduce waste and improve rates), and operate (set targets, automate and improve continuously).

Why does cloud spending need new governance compared with on-premises?

Because anyone with permission can create resources instantly and costs are variable, so control must be continuous rather than only at purchase time.
