Business intelligence with Looker and Looker Studio

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Business intelligence (BI) is the practice of turning data into reports, dashboards and answers that people across a business can use to make decisions. Data in a warehouse only creates value when someone can see and act on it, so BI is the final step of many data journeys. Google Cloud offers two related tools with different purposes.

Looker is an enterprise BI and data platform. Its core idea is a semantic layer, written in a modeling language called LookML, where data teams define business entities and metrics once, for example what 'active customer' or 'net revenue' means and how it is calculated. Everyone who explores data, builds a dashboard or asks a question then uses those same definitions, so different departments get consistent numbers. Looker queries the database directly, such as BigQuery, instead of copying data into its own store, so results are current and access rules are enforced. It also supports scheduled reports, alerts and embedding analytics in other applications.

Looker Studio (formerly Data Studio) is a free, self-service tool for building interactive dashboards and reports. It connects to many sources, including BigQuery, Google Sheets, Google Analytics and Google Ads, and is easy for business users to start with. Reports can be shared like Google Docs. A paid Looker Studio Pro edition adds enterprise management features.

The choice depends on scale and governance needs. A marketing analyst who needs a quick dashboard from a spreadsheet can use Looker Studio in minutes. A large company that needs trusted, consistent metrics across hundreds of users and teams, with governed access, will use Looker.

BI connects with the rest of the platform: data arrives through Pub/Sub, Dataflow or transfer services, is stored in BigQuery, and is presented in Looker or Looker Studio. Increasingly, AI assistants let users ask questions in natural language, but they rely on the same well-defined data underneath.

Key terms

Business intelligence (BI)
Tools and practices that turn data into reports and dashboards for decision making.
Semantic layer
A shared definition of business metrics and data relationships used by all reports.
LookML
Looker's modeling language for defining dimensions, measures and relationships.
Looker Studio
Google's free, self-service dashboard and reporting tool.
Real-world example

Sales and finance at a software company report different 'annual recurring revenue' numbers. The data team defines the metric once in LookML on top of BigQuery, and both teams' Looker dashboards now show the same figure.

Exam tip: Consistent, governed metrics across the enterprise point to Looker and its semantic model. A quick, free dashboard from many sources points to Looker Studio.

Check yourself

What problem does Looker's semantic layer solve?

Inconsistent metrics: definitions are written once in LookML and reused everywhere, so reports agree.

Name two data sources Looker Studio can connect to.

For example BigQuery, Google Sheets, Google Analytics or Google Ads.
