Pricing models and discounts: pay-as-you-go, sustained use discounts, committed use discounts and Spot VMs

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Google Cloud's default pricing is pay-as-you-go: you pay for the resources you use, with no up-front commitment, and most compute is billed per second. This suits new, variable or unpredictable workloads. Google Cloud also offers ways to pay less when your usage has particular patterns, and the exam expects you to match each to a scenario.

Sustained use discounts are applied automatically to certain Compute Engine resources that run for a significant portion of the billing month. The longer they run in the month, the bigger the discount. No commitment or sign-up is needed, but they apply only to some machine types, and newer machine families may not receive them.

Committed use discounts (CUDs) give a lower price in exchange for committing to use a certain amount of resources, or to spend a certain amount, for one or three years. Three-year commitments give larger discounts than one-year ones. Resource-based commitments cover specific amounts of vCPU and memory in a region, while spend-based commitments apply to services such as Cloud SQL or Cloud Run. CUDs suit steady, predictable workloads that you are confident will keep running, such as production databases. The trade-off is that you pay for the commitment even if you use less.

Spot VMs use Google Cloud's spare capacity at a large discount compared with standard VMs. The catch is that Google can stop (preempt) them at any time when it needs the capacity back, with only a short warning. They suit fault-tolerant, flexible work such as batch processing, rendering, testing, and some data analysis or ML training that can checkpoint and resume. They are not suitable for workloads that must run without interruption, such as a primary database or a customer-facing service without redundancy. Preemptible VMs were the earlier version of this option.

Other cost levers include rightsizing with recommendations, autoscaling so capacity follows demand, choosing the right storage class, scaling serverless services to zero, and choosing lower-priced regions when location does not matter.

Key terms

Pay-as-you-go
Paying only for resources used, with no up-front commitment.
Sustained use discount
An automatic discount for certain Compute Engine resources that run for much of a month.
Committed use discount (CUD)
A lower price in exchange for a one- or three-year commitment to resources or spend.
Spot VM
A heavily discounted VM using spare capacity that Google can stop at any time.
Real-world example

A media company runs its always-on production database under a three-year committed use discount, renders video overnight on Spot VMs that can restart if interrupted, and runs its variable web front end on autoscaled pay-as-you-go instances.

Exam tip: Steady and predictable for years: committed use discounts. Fault-tolerant and interruptible: Spot VMs. Automatic, no commitment: sustained use discounts. Unpredictable: pay-as-you-go.

Check yourself

Why is a Spot VM a poor choice for a primary database?

Google can stop Spot VMs at any time, which would interrupt a service that must stay available.

What must a customer give in exchange for a committed use discount?

A commitment to use a set amount of resources or spend for one or three years, paid even if usage is lower.
