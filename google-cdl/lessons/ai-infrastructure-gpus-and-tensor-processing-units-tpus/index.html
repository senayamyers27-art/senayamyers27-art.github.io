AI infrastructure: GPUs and Tensor Processing Units (TPUs)

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Training and running modern machine learning models, especially large deep learning and generative models, requires enormous amounts of computation, mostly large matrix and vector math. General-purpose CPUs can do this work, but slowly. Specialized hardware called accelerators speeds it up dramatically, and access to accelerators at scale is one reason organizations use the cloud for AI.

Graphics processing units (GPUs) were originally designed to render images, which also involves massive parallel math. They turned out to be excellent for ML, and they are widely supported by frameworks such as PyTorch and TensorFlow. Google Cloud offers several types of GPU that you can attach to Compute Engine VMs, use in GKE clusters or use through Vertex AI training and prediction.

Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) are custom chips that Google designed specifically for machine learning. They are application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs), meaning they are built for one kind of workload rather than general computing. Google developed TPUs to run its own AI services, such as Search and Translate, efficiently, and it uses them to train its Gemini models. In Google Cloud, TPUs are available through Vertex AI, GKE and Compute Engine, and they can be connected in large groups, called pods, for training very large models.

Which to use depends on the model, framework and cost. GPUs are very flexible and broadly supported. TPUs can offer strong performance and cost efficiency for large-scale training and serving of models that are suited to them. Many teams try both. For business leaders, the key point is that Google Cloud offers a range of AI infrastructure, including its own TPUs, without the need to buy and run expensive specialized hardware.

You rarely need accelerators when using pre-trained APIs or generative models through Vertex AI, because Google runs that infrastructure for you. Accelerators matter when you train or host your own models.

Key terms

Accelerator
Specialized hardware, such as a GPU or TPU, that speeds up ML computation.
GPU
Graphics processing unit: a processor with many parallel cores, widely used for ML.
TPU
Tensor Processing Unit: Google's custom ASIC designed for machine learning workloads.
ASIC
Application-specific integrated circuit: a chip designed for one type of task.
Real-world example

A biotech startup needs to train a large protein model for a few weeks. Instead of buying hardware, it rents TPUs through Vertex AI for the training run, then serves the finished model on smaller GPU instances, paying only while each is in use.

Exam tip: TPUs are Google's custom-designed ML chips. If a question asks what Google built specifically to accelerate machine learning, the answer is TPUs, not GPUs.

Check yourself

What is a TPU?

A Tensor Processing Unit, Google's custom ASIC designed to accelerate machine learning training and inference.

Why do you rarely need to think about accelerators when using a pre-trained API?

Google runs and manages the underlying infrastructure; you just call the API.
