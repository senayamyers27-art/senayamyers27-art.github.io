Grounding, agents and AI-powered search and conversation for business

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Foundation models are trained on huge amounts of public data up to a point in time. On their own, they do not know a company's internal policies, product catalog or yesterday's prices, and when they lack information they may produce fluent but incorrect answers, called hallucinations. Business uses of generative AI therefore depend on connecting models to the right data and actions.

Grounding means connecting a model's responses to trusted, verifiable sources, so answers are based on real information. A common technique is retrieval-augmented generation (RAG): when a user asks a question, the system first searches a knowledge source, such as company documents, a database or the web, retrieves the most relevant passages, and gives them to the model along with the question. The model then answers using that content and can cite it. Grounding improves accuracy, keeps answers current without retraining the model, and makes responses easier to check.

Enterprise search applies the same ideas to finding information. Instead of matching keywords, AI-powered search understands the meaning of a question and can return a direct, summarized answer from a company's websites, documents and data, with links to sources. Google Cloud provides services in Vertex AI to build this kind of search and conversational experience over your own content without building the retrieval system yourself.

An AI agent goes a step further. An agent uses a model to reason about a goal, plan steps and take actions through tools and APIs, such as looking up an order, updating a ticket or booking an appointment, then checks the result. Customer service agents, employee help desks and coding assistants are common examples. Because agents act, they need clear limits, permissions and human oversight for sensitive actions.

Conversational AI for contact centers combines these pieces: understanding what a customer says, answering from trusted content, handing off to people with a summary, and helping human agents with suggested replies.

Key terms

Hallucination
A confident but incorrect or made-up output from a generative model.
Grounding
Connecting model outputs to trusted sources so responses are based on real information.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
Retrieving relevant content from a knowledge source and giving it to the model with the question.
AI agent
A system that uses a model to plan and take actions through tools to achieve a goal.
Real-world example

An airline builds a customer assistant grounded in its current baggage and refund policies. When a traveler asks about a delayed flight, the agent retrieves the right policy, checks the booking through an API, and offers rebooking options, handing off to a human for unusual cases.

Exam tip: When a scenario describes made-up or outdated answers from a chatbot about company information, the fix is grounding the model in trusted company data, not adding more compute.

Check yourself

How does retrieval-augmented generation reduce hallucinations?

It retrieves relevant trusted content and gives it to the model, so the answer is based on that content rather than only on the model's training data.

What makes an AI agent different from a chatbot that only answers questions?

An agent plans and takes actions through tools and APIs to complete tasks, not just generating text.
