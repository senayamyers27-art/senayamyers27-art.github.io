Artificial intelligence, machine learning and generative AI: definitions and differences

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

These three terms are often used interchangeably, but they are nested ideas, and the exam expects you to tell them apart.

Artificial intelligence (AI) is the broad field of building computer systems that perform tasks normally requiring human intelligence, such as understanding language, recognizing images, making decisions or solving problems. Early AI often relied on hand-written rules: if the customer says 'refund', route to billing. Rules work when the logic is clear, but they break down when the problem has too many cases to write down.

Machine learning (ML) is a subset of AI in which systems learn patterns from data instead of being programmed with explicit rules. You give a model many examples, such as thousands of emails labeled spam or not spam, and a training process adjusts the model until it can make accurate predictions on new examples it has never seen. The main kinds include supervised learning (learning from labeled examples to predict a label or number), unsupervised learning (finding structure such as clusters in unlabeled data) and reinforcement learning (learning by trial and reward). Common business uses are classification, forecasting, recommendations and anomaly detection.

Deep learning is a type of ML that uses neural networks with many layers. It is especially good at unstructured data such as images, audio and text, and it powers most modern AI.

Generative AI is a type of deep learning that creates new content, such as text, images, code, audio or video, rather than only predicting a label. It is built on foundation models: very large models trained on broad data that can be adapted to many tasks. Large language models (LLMs), such as Google's Gemini models, are foundation models for language. You interact with them through a prompt, an instruction or question written in natural language.

In short: AI is the goal, ML is the most common way to reach it, deep learning is a powerful kind of ML, and generative AI is deep learning that produces new content.

Key terms

Artificial intelligence (AI)
The field of building systems that perform tasks that normally need human intelligence.
Machine learning (ML)
A subset of AI where systems learn patterns from data instead of following hand-written rules.
Generative AI
AI that creates new content, such as text, images or code, from a prompt.
Foundation model
A large model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many tasks; LLMs are one kind.
Real-world example

An online retailer uses a rules engine to apply shipping prices (not ML), an ML model to predict which customers may cancel their subscription (predictive ML), and a generative AI model to draft personalized product descriptions (generative AI).

Exam tip: If the system produces new content from a prompt, it is generative AI. If it predicts a category or number from past examples, it is traditional (predictive) ML. If it follows fixed if-then logic, it is not ML at all.

Check yourself

How does machine learning differ from traditional programming?

Traditional programs follow rules written by people; ML models learn the rules or patterns from example data.

What is a foundation model?

A large model trained on broad data that can be adapted to many downstream tasks, such as an LLM.
