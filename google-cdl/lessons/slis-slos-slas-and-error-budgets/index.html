SLIs, SLOs, SLAs and error budgets

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026

Reliability needs to be measured and agreed, not just hoped for. Site Reliability Engineering uses a small set of terms that the exam tests directly. They build on one another.

A service level indicator (SLI) is a carefully chosen measurement of some aspect of the service that users care about. Common SLIs are availability (the proportion of successful requests), latency (the proportion of requests faster than a threshold, such as 300 ms), and correctness or freshness of data. An SLI is a number you measure, for example: 99.95% of requests succeeded in the last 30 days.

A service level objective (SLO) is the target value for an SLI over a period of time, for example: 99.9% of requests will succeed over a rolling 30 days. SLOs are internal goals chosen by the service owner, based on what users actually need. Aiming for 100% is almost never right: it is extremely expensive, slows change, and users usually cannot tell the difference beyond a certain point because their own devices and networks are less reliable.

A service level agreement (SLA) is a formal contract with customers that states a level of service and the consequences, typically financial credits, if the provider fails to meet it. Google Cloud publishes SLAs for many services. SLAs are usually looser than internal SLOs, so a team notices and fixes problems before a contractual breach.

The error budget is the amount of unreliability an SLO allows: 100% minus the SLO. With a 99.9% availability SLO, the error budget is 0.1% of requests, or about 43 minutes of full downtime in 30 days. The error budget turns reliability into a shared decision tool. While budget remains, the team can release new features and take reasonable risks. If the budget is used up, the team slows or freezes feature releases and focuses on reliability until the service is back within its objective. This aligns developers and operators instead of pitting speed against stability.

Key terms

SLI
Service level indicator: a measurement of service behavior, such as the percentage of successful requests.
SLO
Service level objective: an internal target for an SLI over a period of time.
SLA
Service level agreement: a contract with customers stating consequences if a service level is not met.
Error budget
The allowed unreliability under an SLO, equal to 100% minus the SLO.
Real-world example

A payments API has an SLO of 99.95% successful requests over 30 days. Halfway through the month, a bad release uses 80% of the error budget. The team pauses new feature launches, adds automated rollback and resumes releases once reliability is back on track.

Exam tip: SLI is what you measure, SLO is your internal target, SLA is the external contract with penalties. Error budget = 100% minus SLO. SLAs are usually looser than SLOs.

Check yourself

A service's SLO is 99.9% availability. What is its error budget?

0.1% of requests (or time) in the measurement period.

Why should an SLA usually be looser than the internal SLO?

So the team detects and fixes problems when it misses its internal target, before breaching the customer contract.
