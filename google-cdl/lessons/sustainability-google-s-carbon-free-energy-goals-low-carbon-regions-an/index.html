Sustainability: Google's carbon-free energy goals, low-carbon regions and the Carbon Footprint tool

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed September 25, 2026 · Leer en español

Data centers use large amounts of electricity, so IT has a real environmental impact. Many organizations now have sustainability targets and must report their emissions, including those from cloud services. Moving to an efficient cloud and choosing low-carbon options can reduce a company's IT footprint, and the exam includes sustainability as part of operating in the cloud.

Google has a long record in this area. It became carbon neutral for its operations in 2007 through a combination of efficiency, renewable energy and carbon offsets (it has since shifted its focus from offsets toward cutting emissions directly), and since 2017 it has purchased enough renewable energy each year to match its global annual electricity use. Google's goal is to run on 24/7 carbon-free energy on every grid where it operates by 2030, meaning every hour of electricity use is matched by carbon-free sources on the same grid, which is more demanding than annual matching. It also has a goal to reach net-zero emissions across its operations and value chain by 2030. Google designs its data centers for high energy efficiency, including custom hardware and machine learning used to optimize cooling.

Google Cloud gives customers tools to make lower-carbon choices. The carbon-free energy percentage (CFE%) of each region is published, showing how much of the electricity used there comes from carbon-free sources on an hourly basis. In the console, regions with the lowest grid carbon intensity are marked with a low CO2 indicator, so teams can choose them for new workloads when location requirements allow. Batch jobs that are not time-sensitive can also be scheduled in lower-carbon regions.

The Carbon Footprint tool, available in the console for free, reports the estimated greenhouse gas emissions associated with a customer's Google Cloud usage, broken down by project, region and product. Organizations use it for sustainability reporting and to track the effect of their choices. Data can be exported to BigQuery for analysis.

Efficient practices also reduce emissions: rightsizing, autoscaling, deleting idle resources and using serverless services that scale to zero save both cost and energy.

Key terms

24/7 carbon-free energy
Matching every hour of electricity use with carbon-free sources on the same grid; Google's 2030 goal.
Carbon-free energy percentage (CFE%)
The share of a region's hourly electricity use that comes from carbon-free sources.
Carbon Footprint tool
A console tool that reports the estimated emissions of a customer's Google Cloud usage.
Low CO2 region
A region marked in the console as having low grid carbon intensity.
Real-world example

A European retailer must report IT emissions each year. It uses the Carbon Footprint tool to see emissions by project, moves a nightly batch analytics job to a region marked low CO2 that also meets its data residency rules, and deletes idle development VMs, reducing both its bill and its reported emissions.

Exam tip: Know the tools: the Carbon Footprint tool reports customer emissions; low CO2 region indicators and CFE% help choose regions. Google's 2030 goal is 24/7 carbon-free energy.

Check yourself

What does the Carbon Footprint tool show?

Estimated greenhouse gas emissions from a customer's Google Cloud usage, broken down by project, region and product.

How can a team reduce emissions for a batch job with no location requirement?

Run it in a region marked low CO2 or with a high carbon-free energy percentage, and size it efficiently.
