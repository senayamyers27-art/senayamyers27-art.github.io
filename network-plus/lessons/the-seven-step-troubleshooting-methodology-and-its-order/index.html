CompTIA Network+ N10-009 · Domain 5: Network troubleshooting

The seven-step troubleshooting methodology and its order

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 6, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Troubleshooting means finding and fixing what is wrong, and CompTIA teaches a seven-step recipe so you do not guess wildly. First, figure out exactly what the problem is by asking questions. Second, come up with a likely cause. Third, check whether that guess is right. Fourth, plan the fix and think about who it might affect. Fifth, carry out the fix, or hand it to someone more experienced. Sixth, make sure everything really works and stop it from happening again. Seventh, write down what happened and what you did. It is like a doctor: ask about symptoms, suspect a cause, run a test, plan treatment, treat, check the patient is better, and update the medical record. Skipping steps leads to wrong fixes and repeat problems.

It is 10:20 a.m. at Silverline Property Management, and three tickets arrive within minutes: accounting cannot print, the third floor has no internet, and a remote worker says the VPN is slow. Kai, the newest technician, feels the pressure and starts rebooting the third-floor switch before asking anyone a single question. The reboot drops the phones for five minutes, and the internet problem is still there afterward. His team lead, Rosa, pulls him aside. She does not scold him; she draws seven boxes on the whiteboard and asks him which box he skipped, and which box he should be in right now. What are the seven boxes, and why does the order matter so much?

CompTIA's troubleshooting methodology is a structured way to solve problems so you do not jump to conclusions, make things worse, or fix the symptom but not the cause. Experienced engineers often move through it quickly and informally, but the structure keeps you honest when a problem is stressful or unfamiliar. The exam tests the steps and, above all, their order, often by describing what a technician has just done and asking what should come next. Learn the seven steps word for word: identify the problem; establish a theory of probable cause; test the theory to determine the cause; establish a plan of action to resolve the problem and identify potential effects; implement the solution or escalate as necessary; verify full system functionality and, if applicable, implement preventive measures; and document findings, actions, outcomes and lessons learned.
