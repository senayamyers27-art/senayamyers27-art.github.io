CompTIA Network+ N10-009 · Domain 3: Network operations

Change management and configuration management: baselines, golden configs, backups

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 6, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Most network outages are caused by people changing things, not by broken hardware. Change management is a simple agreement: before you change something important, you write down what you will do, why, what could go wrong and how you will undo it, and someone else approves it. Configuration management means always knowing how every device is supposed to be set up and keeping saved copies of those settings. Then, when something breaks, you can compare today with yesterday and put things back. It is like renovating a kitchen: you get a plan approved, you schedule the work for when nobody is cooking, and you keep the old drawings in case you need to restore the plumbing.

At 6:15 a.m. your phone buzzes. Nobody at Lakeside Freight's main warehouse can reach the inventory servers, and the trucks leave at seven. You log in and learn that Omar, on the night shift, added a firewall rule at midnight to fix a vendor's connection. It worked, he went home, and somewhere in the rule order the warehouse VLAN got blocked. Omar is asleep. There is no ticket, no record of what the rules looked like yesterday, and no written way to undo the change. You have forty-five minutes. What should have existed before Omar typed his first command?

Many outages are caused not by hardware failure but by changes: a mistyped command, an untested upgrade, a firewall rule added in a hurry. Change management and configuration management are the processes that keep changes controlled and configurations known and recoverable. They are sometimes dismissed as paperwork, but their real purpose is fewer surprises, faster recovery and a clear record of what changed when something breaks. Network+ tests the steps of the change process and the vocabulary of baselines, golden configurations and backups.
