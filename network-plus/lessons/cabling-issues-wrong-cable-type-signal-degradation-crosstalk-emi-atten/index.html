CompTIA Network+ N10-009 · Domain 5: Network troubleshooting

Cabling issues: wrong cable type, signal degradation, crosstalk, EMI, attenuation, improper termination, TX/RX transposed

Explain it simpler

Network cables carry signals, and those signals can get weak, noisy or mixed up. Using the wrong kind of cable is like putting the wrong fuel in a car: it may not run at all, or run badly. Signals fade over long distances (attenuation), so copper Ethernet has a 100-meter limit. Nearby electrical equipment, like motors and fluorescent lights, can add electrical noise (EMI). Wires inside one cable can leak signal into each other (crosstalk), especially if they are untwisted too much at the plug. Badly attached connectors cause flaky links. And fiber needs one strand to send and the other to receive; if the two ends are not crossed correctly, the link never comes up. Matching the symptom to the cause saves you from replacing good equipment.

At Ironwood Fabrication, the new fiber uplink to the paint shop has been dark since it was installed this morning. Both optics are brand new, the right type and the right wavelength, and the installer swears the fiber is perfect. Across the plant, the shipping office's PC works most of the day but crawls every afternoon when the big compressor kicks on, and the warehouse office at the end of a very long copper run never gets more than a sputtering connection. Lena, the network technician, has already been told twice to just replace the switch. Before she spends money on hardware, she wonders: could all three problems be the cables, and what would each one look like?

Physical layer problems are common and are often mistaken for higher-layer faults. A cable problem can cause no link at all, a link that comes up at a lower speed than expected, a link that flaps up and down, or a link that works but produces errors and slow performance. Because so much depends on Layer 1, a bottom-up check of cabling is often the fastest route to a fix. Knowing the symptoms of each cabling issue lets you pick the right fix and the right test tool.
