CompTIA Network+ N10-009 · Domain 3: Network operations

Life-cycle management: end of life/support, software and firmware management, decommissioning

▶ Watch the overview video

Last reviewed October 6, 2026 · Leer en español

Explain it simpler

Network equipment does not last forever. It gets bought, installed, kept up to date, and eventually replaced. The maker of a device promises to fix problems in it for a few years. After a set date, they stop sending fixes, so new security holes in it stay open forever. That is why teams track these dates and plan replacements early. While a device is in use, its built-in software needs careful updating: read what the update changes, check the download is genuine, save a copy of the settings, and keep a way to go back. When a device is retired, its memory must be wiped, because it can hold passwords and network secrets. It is like selling an old phone: you would erase it first, not just toss it in a drawer.

It is Tuesday morning at Cedar Valley Clinic, and an auditor named Priya slides a printout across the table toward you. Two firewalls guarding the patient records network are highlighted in yellow. Their vendor stopped publishing security patches for this model fourteen months ago, and nobody noticed because the boxes kept running without complaint. In the corner of the server room sits a pallet of retired switches waiting for a recycler to pick them up, still holding their old configurations. Priya asks two questions. Why is unsupported equipment still guarding sensitive data, and what exactly is on those switches before they leave the building?

Every network device moves through a life cycle: planning and procurement, deployment, operation and maintenance, and finally retirement. Managing that cycle deliberately keeps the network secure and supported, spreads costs predictably across budgets, and avoids the unpleasant surprise of an old device failing when no replacement part, patch or support contract exists. Life-cycle management is tightly connected to the asset inventory, change management and security. Network+ tests it mainly through scenarios about unsupported devices, upgrades and safe disposal.
