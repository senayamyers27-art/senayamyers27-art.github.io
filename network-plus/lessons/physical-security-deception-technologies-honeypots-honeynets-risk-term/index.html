Physical security, deception technologies (honeypots, honeynets), risk terms, audits and compliance (PCI DSS, GDPR)

Explain it simpler

Security is not only about software. If someone can walk up to a switch or carry off a hard drive, passwords do not help much, so locks, badges and cameras come first. Some defenders also set out fake systems, like a decoy safe in a store, that no honest person ever touches; if anyone does, an alarm goes off. To decide what to protect first, teams use a few simple words: a weakness (vulnerability), something that could use that weakness (threat), and how likely and how bad the result would be (risk). Finally, some rules are required. Any business that takes credit cards must follow the card industry's security standard, called PCI DSS, and anyone handling personal data about people in the European Union must follow the EU privacy law called GDPR.

It is Monday morning at Lakeshore Inn & Suites, and Dana from IT is walking an auditor through the server closet. The auditor stops at the door: it is propped open with a box of printer paper. Inside, a switch port blinks next to a cable nobody recognizes, and the visitor log has no entries since Friday. Back at her desk, Dana sees a weekend alert from the decoy reservation database that no real employee ever uses. The auditor asks three questions in a row: who could have walked in, how would you know, and does any of this put the card payment system out of compliance? Dana realizes she needs one clear way to think about doors, decoys, risk and regulations together. Where would you start?

Physical security is the foundation layer of defense in depth. If an attacker can touch your equipment, many logical controls can be bypassed: they can plug a laptop into a switch, reset a router to factory defaults through its console port, install a hardware keylogger between a keyboard and a PC, or simply walk out with a server or a backup drive. Network+ pairs this topic with deception technologies that detect intruders, the vocabulary of risk, and the audits and compliance frameworks that require all of these controls, so it helps to see them as one connected story.
