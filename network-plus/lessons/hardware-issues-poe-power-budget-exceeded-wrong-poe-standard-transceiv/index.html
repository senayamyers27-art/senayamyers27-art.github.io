CompTIA Network+ N10-009 · Domain 5: Network troubleshooting

Hardware issues: PoE power budget exceeded, wrong PoE standard, transceiver mismatch, signal strength

Explain it simpler

Network gear needs three things to work well: enough electricity, parts that match, and a signal strong enough to understand. Many devices, such as cameras and wireless access points, get their electricity through the network cable from the switch. That is called Power over Ethernet. The switch has only so much power to share, like a power strip that trips if you plug in too many heaters. If you run out, some devices stay dark or keep restarting. Devices can also ask for more power than an older switch knows how to give. Plug-in modules at each end of a fiber cable must match, like two people speaking the same language. And radio or light signals that are too weak arrive garbled. Check these simple things before deciding a device is broken.

It is Monday morning at Pinecrest Logistics, and Dana from facilities has installed eight new security cameras over the weekend. Your phone buzzes before coffee: three of the cameras show nothing, two keep rebooting, and the lobby access point that worked fine on Friday now offers only a weak 2.4 GHz signal. Dana is sure the cameras are defective and wants to ship them back. Meanwhile the warehouse fiber uplink that was swapped last week is logging errors every few minutes. Nothing looks obviously broken, every light is on somewhere, and yet half the new hardware is misbehaving. Is this really a box of bad devices, or is something about power, modules or signal quietly getting in the way?

Some network problems come from the hardware itself: not enough power, incompatible modules, or signals too weak (or occasionally too strong) to be understood. These issues often make a device look dead, unstable or only partly working, which tempts people to replace it rather than find the cause. Knowing the patterns saves time and money. The CompTIA Network+ exam presents them as short scenarios with one telling detail, such as 'only some cameras power on' or 'the link came up at one end but not the other', and expects you to connect that detail to the right cause.
